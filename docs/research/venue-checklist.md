# World venue groupings: a checklist

A ranked list of the stadium groupings worth drawing with the district scenes ([`mode: "neighbours"`](../district.md) for venues side by side, `mode: "city"` for venues spread over a city) and of the famous single venues that belong beside them. It is a planning page: nothing here is drawn yet except what the status board says. Written on 20 October 2026; facts were read that day; sources and their limits are at the end.

- **233 rows over 514 venues**: 95 neighbours groupings, 75 city groupings and 63 single venues, in seven regions. Priority 1 is 43 rows, priority 2 is 105, priority 3 is 85.
- **Status marks.** ☑ done, ◐ in progress, ▶ next, ☐ open. **P1** is worth doing soon (famous, cheap, or both), **P2** is good, **P3** is for completeness.
- **Where to start reading.** The status board, then the [recommendations](#recommendations) and the three [decisions asked for](#the-three-decisions), then the region tables.

## The three decisions

### Japan: Tokyo Dome City first, Koshien second, Meiji Jingu Gaien as the first district

- **Tokyo Dome City first.** The Dome (Wikidata [Q733748](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q733748), 55,000) and Korakuen Hall ([Q4389857](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q4389857)) are both OSM ways with `building=yes` ([way 20028328](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/20028328), [way 45977418](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/45977418)), 140 m apart; the Dome is already a package ballpark (`tokyo-dome`). It is the quickest Japanese scene and the most famous domed stadium in the country; Korakuen Hall adds boxing and pro wrestling.
- **Koshien second.** [Q959811](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q959811), 60,000 by Wikidata, the home of the spring and summer high school tournaments and of the Hanshin Tigers, also already a package park (`hanshin-koshien-stadium`, with Japanese names). OSM has it as [relation 9845926](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/9845926) tagged `leisure=stadium` and no building tag, so the building and roof need the lookup BC Place needed. More work than the Dome, but it is the single most famous Japanese ballpark, so it is worth the extra step.
- **Then Meiji Jingu Gaien** as the first Japanese district: National Stadium ([Q14862239](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q14862239), 68,000, relation 10071391 with `building=stadium`), Meiji Jingu Stadium ([Q944559](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q944559)), Chichibunomiya Rugby Stadium ([Q631237](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q631237)) and the Tokyo Metropolitan Gymnasium ([Q1066903](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q1066903)), 940 m across with 240 to 280 m between neighbours. Japan's National Stadium is the Japanese word *kyōgijō* that the package is named for, which is a nice reason to include it.
- **Single halls to follow:** Ryōgoku Kokugikan (sumo) and Nippon Budokan (the budō), both of which the package already has the floors for.

### The east coast of Canada: Montreal first, then Quebec City, then Halifax

- **Montreal first.** Bell Centre ([Q522282](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q522282), 21,273, [way 19911284](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/19911284) `building=stadium`) arrives with the NHL Atlantic division anyway, and the Olympic Stadium ([Q285467](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q285467), [way 108505523](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/108505523) `building=stadium`) is the most distinctive stadium shape in Canada. The city scene shows the two 7 km apart, with Molson Stadium and IGA Stadium; the Olympic Park neighbours scene (Olympic Stadium, Stade Saputo, Maurice Richard Arena, 600 m across) is the second step.
- **Quebec City second.** Centre Vidéotron ([Q738388](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q738388), way 299757371 `building=yes`) and the Colisée ([Q600226](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q600226), way 41167716 `building=yes`) are 240 m apart, both building ways: the Nordiques' old and new homes.
- **Halifax third.** Scotiabank Centre ([Q976408](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q976408), way 553574575 `building=yes`, 10,595) and Wanderers Grounds ([Q7967195](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q7967195), tagged `leisure=park`, 7,244) are 700 m apart. It is the east coast in name but the venues are small and the grounds have no building outline, so it ranks below the other two.

### The New York area: Flushing Meadows first, then MSG, then Meadowlands, then the whole city

- **Flushing Meadows first as a grouping.** Citi Field ([Q1072882](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q1072882)), Arthur Ashe Stadium ([Q609551](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q609551), way 284859611 `building=stadium`) and Louis Armstrong Stadium ([Q47487977](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q47487977), way 840573223 `building=yes`) lie within 800 m of one another. It is the only real neighbours cluster among New York's big venues and it joins baseball with tennis. Etihad Park, a soccer stadium for NYCFC and Gotham FC, is under construction next to Citi Field (Wikipedia, revision 1379199098), so the scene will gain a third stadium later.
- **Madison Square Garden second, and as a single.** [Q186125](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q186125), 20,789, [way 138141251](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/138141251) `building=yes`, already a package arena (`madison-square-garden`). It has no neighbour that is a major venue, so it is a single, and it comes with the NHL release that carries the Rangers (the order of the later divisions is not known).
- **Meadowlands third.** MetLife Stadium ([Q10862290](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q10862290), 82,500, 2026 World Cup final) and the closed Meadowlands Arena ([Q538218](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q538218)) are 620 m apart across a highway.
- **The whole New York city scene last.** Nine venues over about 37 km (MSG, Barclays Center, Yankee Stadium, Citi Field, Arthur Ashe Stadium, UBS Arena, MetLife Stadium, Prudential Center, Sports Illustrated Stadium): it needs nine outlines, but four of them (MSG, Barclays, UBS Arena, Prudential Center) are package arenas and five carry building tags in OSM (one of them a relation).
- Yankee Stadium, Barclays Center and UBS Arena are each far from the others and are best seen only in the city scene or as singles.

## How this was made, and what to trust

Read on 2026-10-20, a request at a time.

- **Wikipedia titles to Wikidata items.** The English Wikipedia API (`prop=pageprops`, redirects followed) gave each venue's Wikidata item; Wikidata's `wbgetentities` gave its coordinates (P625) and capacity statements (P1083). Where a title redirected, the venue is listed under the name the checklist used and the article is linked in the venue data. Wikidata is CC0.
- **Distances** are great-circle distances between the Wikidata coordinates of the venues of a row. Some coordinates mark the middle of a site, not a building, so read them as good to a hundred metres or so. A row's "nearest" figures are each venue's distance to its closest neighbour. A neighbours row marked "wide" has gaps of a kilometre or more, which reads better as a city scene.
- **OpenStreetMap.** One Overpass query per 30 venues (about 19 queries, 15 seconds apart, a User-Agent that names the project) asked for every node, way and relation whose `wikidata` tag is exactly the venue's item. "Building" means a tagged way or relation has a `building` tag, which is what the real-venue route uses. "Area only" means tagged ways or relations exist but carry only `leisure=stadium`, `leisure=park` or similar; the roof or building of such a stadium is often a separate object with no Wikidata tag, as BC Place's is, so "area only" is a lookup, not a dead end. "None" means nothing is tagged with the item (a building may still exist, untagged). The eight Pacific arenas were used as a check: the objects found match the ways the real venues use. Credit: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0. Only object numbers and tag names are kept here, no geometry.
- **Sports, leagues, tenants, opening years and the notes in "Why notable"** are from general knowledge current to mid-2026 and were not each re-checked. A "(?)" or the word "unverified" marks the ones that are least certain. Facts read from a Wikipedia article for this page name the revision (Etihad Park, Camp Nou, Meadowlands Arena, Stadium 974, Choctaw Stadium, Desert Diamond Arena, Oakland Coliseum, Sports Illustrated Stadium, Allegiant Stadium were read on 2026-10-20). Stadium names change often (sponsors); the checklist uses the Wikidata or Wikipedia name of 2026-10-20 and mentions the former one where it is still common.
- **Capacity** is the largest P1083 statement on the item, so it can be a concert or expanded figure, not the seats for a sport; the real-venue route reads seats for the sport, as [the real venues page](../venues.md) describes.
- **"In the package today"** compares each venue's Wikidata item with `docs/arenas.md`, `docs/grounds.md` and `src/parks.data.ts` at release 0.27.0. The package's item and the one Wikipedia gives can differ for a few venues, which would show as "none" for a venue it does hold.
- **Out of scope.** Golf courses (Augusta and the rest: the package has no golf surface), motor-racing circuits (Indianapolis Motor Speedway, the Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez, Yas Marina and others), horse-racing courses (Flemington, Belmont Park) and arenas with no sport the package draws are left out or named only where they stand beside a venue that is in. Add them only if a surface for the sport exists.
- **Not covered.** Past venues (Boston Garden, the old Wembley, Tiger Stadium of Detroit) are not in the list; the package's `PARKS` and `ARENAS` hold many of them, and a historical scene is a separate question. Russia's Sochi and Moscow groupings are marked only as venues of past Games and tournaments; a reader may prefer to leave them out.
