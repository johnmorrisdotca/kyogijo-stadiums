# Multi-surface events: what runs several at once

Research for the tournament layouts, written on 19 October 2026. The question: which other surfaces are laid out several to a hall, a stadium or a site for one event, with a real layout and a published rule behind the spacing? The table tennis, pickleball, curling and judo layouts are being built separately and are not researched here; this page covers everything else.

How to read it:

- **Grid** is rows by columns: `1x4` is one row of four, `2x3` is two rows of three.
- **In the package** says whether [the surface](../surfaces.md) is already drawn. Where it is, a tournament layout needs only a preset (the count, the grid, the gap). Where it is not, the surface has to be drawn first, from its own rulebook.
- **Fits** means the surface model of the package: a flat, markable area with lines, drawn to scale from a published rule.
- **Unsourced** means I looked and found no published figure; nothing here is guessed. **Secondary** means the figure comes from a page that quotes a rulebook, not from the rulebook itself.
- Every figure has a link. Nothing is copied: the rules and event pages are summarised, with a short quote only where the wording matters.

## Notes, one sport at a time

### Karate

The WKF's branding manuals for its Karate1 leagues fix the field of play in a few lines, which makes this the closest match to the judo layout with branding on the mats. The Series A manual (2026) says the layout "consists of 6 tatami areas, unless otherwise agreed with the WKF Events Department", requires a light blue or blue carpet, and draws the six areas as two rows of three with a number sign for each (TATAMI 1 to 6) and a city name over each ([Series A branding manual, 2026](https://www.wkf.net/files/pdf/documents/SERIES%20A%20BRANDING%20MANUAL%202026.pdf)). The Youth League manual gives 8 areas, drawn as two rows of four, on a black, dark grey or yellow carpet ([Youth League branding guidelines](https://www.wkf.net/files/pdf/documents/KARATE%20ONE%20YOUTH%20LEAGUE%20-%20BRANDING%20GUIDELINES.pdf), a 25 MB file).

The area itself: the 2024 kumite rules (Article 1.1 to 1.4) give a matted square of 8 m measured from the outside, the outer metre in another colour, a further 2 m matted safety area on all sides that "may be reduced to 1.5 metres" where the hall has no room, no sign, wall or pillar within 1 m of the safety area, and 1.5 m between areas where monitors stand ([WKF kumite rules 2024](https://karateliitto.fi/app/uploads/2024/02/WKF_Kumite_Competition_Rules_2024.pdf)). The 2020 rules give an 8 m matted square with a further 1 m on all sides as a safety area and "a clear safety area of two metres" ([WKF rules 2020](https://archive.teamusa.com/usa-karate/officials-and-coaches/-/media/wkf_competition%20rules_2020_en.pdf)).

- A search summary of the 2026 master copy reported a minimum of 2 m between competition areas. I could not open that file (the WKF site returns an image to a script), so the 2 m is **unverified**; use 1.5 m from the 2024 text until it is read.
- Real events: Karate1 Series A in Kuala Lumpur (Nilai, N9 Arena, 3 to 5 October 2025, 917 athletes from 76 countries, per [karate.news](https://karate.news/en/articles/karate-1-series-a-kuala-lumpur-2025-official-results-and-medal-table)). The area count at that event is **unsourced**; the manual's six is the rule of the league, not a count of that event.
- Fits: yes. `karate` exists; the layout only needs a count, a grid, a gap and a sign.

### Fencing

A piste is 14 m long and 1.5 to 2 m wide, with a centre line, two on-guard lines 2 m either side of it, two rear limit lines 7 m from the centre and the last 2 m before them in another colour (Book 1, t.13 and t.14, in the 2015 restructured text on [static.fie.org](https://static.fie.org/uploads/7/38636-10.2%20technical%20rules%20restructured%20ang.pdf); that text says the field-of-play chapter moved to the host's specifications (the Cahiers des Charges), and the figures are repeated by [the Western Australian sports guide](https://cits.wa.gov.au/sport-and-recreation/sports-dimensions-guide/fencing), secondary). The material rules of August 2026 (m.57) say the platform for a raised piste "must not exceed 50 cm", and be wider than the piste by at least 25 cm each side, with a gentle slope at each end; the conductive piste is made 17 to 18 m long, 1.5 to 2 m longer at each end ([FIE Book 3, August 2026](https://static.fie.org/uploads/40/204157-book%20material%20August%202026%20ang.pdf)).

The hall layouts:

- **Veterans Worlds handbook.** Two models: a hall with 4 coloured pistes, a hall for the final and 4 adjoining pistes; or a hall with 5 coloured pistes with the final piste in the centre, plus 4 adjoining pistes. The 5-piste hall is 4 coloured zones of about 20 m by 8 m and one zone of a different colour about 40 m by 4 m "to come in between the coloured pistes", on which the raised final piste stands. In the 4-piste hall the refereeing zone is at least 3.5 m from the piste and the area is fenced. The handbook's diagrams with the minimum distances are images that I did not read ([FIE veterans handbook](https://static.fie.org/uploads/40/204405-Cahier%20des%20Charges%20-%20CHM%20veterans%20ang%20revisions.pdf), undated).
- Read with the arithmetic, those figures fit `2x2` zones either side of the 40 by 4 m strip: two 20 m zones make 40 m, and 8 + 4 + 8 m make 20 m, so a hall floor of about 40 by 20 m. This is my reading, not a figure the handbook states.
- **Senior Worlds, Budapest 2019** (BOK Sports Hall). Main Hall A is 8,000 m² with a platform of 4 colour pistes and the final podium (5 pistes), Preliminary Halls B and C hold 22 pistes, the oval stadium 12 warm-up pistes, 34 pistes in all ([information for delegations](https://static.fie.org/uploads/22/110279-FIE_WFC_Budapest_2019_Info_for_Delegation_Brochure_A4_03.pdf)). Hall A's floor is the hall's, not the field of play's.
- **Senior Worlds, Moscow 2015.** Main hall "Olimpiysky": 4 colour pistes and the final podium; a preliminary hall of 24 pistes; 33 pistes in all ([information for delegations](https://static.fie.org/uploads/6/32640-MOSCOU-InfoDelegation_V2.pdf)).
- **Junior Worlds, Torun 2019.** A finale hall with the final piste (podium) and 8 coloured pistes, 10 more for preliminaries, a preliminary hall of 13, 31 in use ([information for delegations](https://static.fie.org/uploads/20/104146--WC_Junior_Torun_2019_information%20for%20delegation-2.pdf)).
- Not found: a minimum gap between two pistes, outside the diagrams. **Unsourced.**
- Fits: yes, but a piste is a long thin strip with the platform in the drawing, so it needs the surface first. Recommendation: add first as a new surface; the 5-piste hall (4 around a raised centre) is the layout to build.

### Badminton

The BWF's Specifications for International Standard Facilities (Statutes 5.3.4, in force 14 October 2019) say, for Grade 1 and 2 events and the Games: at least 2 m clear behind the baselines and 1.5 m beside the sidelines; "the 1.5 metre clear space shall also be a minimum requirement between any two courts marked out side by side"; at Grade 1 and 2 "at least four metres" between courts is recommended; competition courts "must always be laid down side by side"; court mats run off 1 m at the back and 0.5 m at the sides; clear height 12 m ([BWF specifications](https://www.badmintonpanam.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/3.3.4-Specs-for-Int_l-Standard-Facilities-14-October-2019-V1.1.pdf)).

The Olympic requirement for Tokyo 2020 asks for a floor of the field of play of at least 48 by 30 m with 12 m of height, equipment for 3 competition courts and 3 warm-up courts, a warm-up area for at least 3 courts, and a practice hall of 8 or more courts ([BWF Statement of Badminton's Requirement, Tokyo 2020](https://www.badmintonpanam.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/3.4.1.3-OG-Statement-of-Badmintons-Requirement-Tokyo-2020.pdf)). It is a requirement for bids, not a record of one event, and it does not give the grid.

- Fits: yes. Always a `1xN` row by the rule; two gaps (1.5 m and 4 m) are worth presets.

### Tennis

Swiss Tennis (a national federation; the page is dated 15 September 2025 and cites the ITF Rules of Tennis and a Swiss planning guide, BASPO 530) lists 3.66 m to the side and 6.40 m behind for a single court; for double courts 7.32 m between two courts; for three or more courts side by side 7.32 m between adjacent courts, and two reductions: at 5.95 m doubles can be played on every second court only, with the court between used for singles, and at 4.58 m doubles on every second court only with no singles between ([Swiss Tennis](https://www.swisstennis.ch/media/12802/tennisplaetze_erforderliche_distanzen_f.pdf)). It also gives the tour minimums (ITF World Tennis Tour 3.66 m to the side and 6.40 m behind; ATP Tour 4.57 m and 8.23 m). Other national guides give different gaps for unfenced club courts (the Western Australian guide has 3.66 m for club courts and 5.48 m for ITF tour courts, [WA sports guide](https://cits.wa.gov.au/sport-and-recreation/sports-dimensions-guide/tennis)), so a preset names which table it follows.

- Real site: Wimbledon has 19 courts at the club, 2 of them show courts, per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Championships,_Wimbledon). That article does not say how they are laid out; the layout of the outside courts is **unsourced**.
- Fits: yes. `tennis` exists; a bank of courts is `1xN` or `2xN` at the chosen gap.

### Beach volleyball

At the 2019 World Championships in Hamburg, Am Rothenbaum had a centre court and two side courts on the first day (8 matches on the centre court, 10 on each side court), per [a release carried by asianvolleyball.net](https://asianvolleyball.net/new/?p=42741). Capacity and the placement of the side courts relative to the stands were not stated in any source I found: **unsourced**. The Rome 2022 Worlds used the centre court of the Foro Italico (10,500 seats per the [FIVB site](https://en.volleyballworld.com/beachvolleyball/competitions/beach-volleyball-world-championships/2022/news/rome-to-host-2022-world-championships-in-june)); the outside courts there are also unsourced.

The court is 16 by 8 m ([FIVB glossary](https://www.fivb.org/EN/Beachvolleyball/Glossary.asp)). A Hong Kong Education Bureau infographic of the FIVB Official Beach Volleyball Rules 2025-2028 gives a free zone of at least 3 m and 5 to 6 m from the lines for FIVB World and Official competitions, and sand at least 40 cm deep; the AVP rulebook gives a free zone of at least 3 m ([AVP](https://avp.com/wp-content/uploads/2017-AVP-Rulebook.pdf), 2017). Both are secondary to the FIVB text, which I did not find as a file. For indoor volleyball an older Western Australian guide lists a free zone of 5 m at the sides and 8 m at the ends for FIVB events ([WA sports guide](https://cits.wa.gov.au/sport-and-recreation/sports-dimensions-guide/volleyball), citing 2013-2016 rules).

- Fits: yes; `volleyball` with `league: "fivb-beach"` exists. The centre court larger than the side courts is a good test of a layout with unequal cells.

### Small-sided football

The FA's Rule 38 (2025-26) recommends pitch sizes by age (in yards, without run-off: 5v5 40 by 30, 7v7 60 by 40, 9v9 80 by 50, 11v11 U13/14 90 by 55, U15/16 100 by 60, U17 and up 110 by 70), a run-off of at least 3 yards all round, and, "where pitches neighbour others within a confined area", a run-off between the two "ideally 6 yards". Under "Multi-line Marking on Single Pitches" it allows several pitches to be marked on one in different colours, of one width of at most 12 cm, and recommends red for 5v5, yellow for 7v7, blue for 9v9 and white for 11v11 ([FA Rule 38](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/files/thefaportal/governance-docs/rules-of-the-association/2025-26/38---goalpost-and-pitch-sizes-and-line-marking.ashx)). The FA's pitch-layout guidance documents (colours and layouts on the FA website) were not read.

- Real event: the Gothia Cup in Gothenburg had 4,349 games on 110 fields in 2017, with 1,730 teams from 82 nations ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothia_Cup)). The article gives no field layout: **unsourced**.
- Fits: yes. This is an overlay (one drawing with several marked pitches, each in its own colour), so it is a second kind of layout next to the grid. Recommendation: second wave.

### Kendo

The FIK Regulations of Kendo Shiai and Shinpan (July 2021 English edition) give a shiai-jo that is "a square or a rectangle with sides of 9 to 11 meters in length inclusive of the width of the line", a space of at least 1.5 m kept outside it, and lines of 5 to 10 cm, white ([regulations](https://kendo.rs/files/202107-Regulations-of-Kendo-Shiai-and-Shinpan.pdf)). I found no event with a count of shiai-jo or a hall plan: **unsourced**. Fits: yes, `kendo` exists. Recommendation: second wave, once an event is sourced.

### Wrestling

The Western Australian guide, citing the 2014 FILA rules, gives a mat of 9 m diameter with a 1.5 m border, an outer protection area, and a platform between 0.5 and 1.1 m high at the Games and Worlds ([WA sports guide](https://cits.wa.gov.au/sport-and-recreation/sports-dimensions-guide/wrestling)). A UWW document for a related style (Qazaq Kuresi) gives a 12 by 12 m mat with a central circle of 9 m ([UWW](https://cdn.uww.org/2026-08/international_kazak_kuresi_rules.pdf), a different discipline, so secondary). I did not find the UWW freestyle and Greco-Roman rulebook as a file.

- Real event: Paris 2024 wrestling, 5 to 11 August. The UWW page says "All the bouts will be held at the Champ-de-Mars Arena" ([UWW](https://uww.org/article/wrestling-2024-paris-olympics-schedule-venue-timings-weight-categories)); Wikipedia says Grand Palais Éphémère ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics)). The sources conflict; the UWW page is the better one. The Olympics.com session pages name Mat A, Mat B and Mat C, which suggests three mats, but I only saw this in search results, not from the pages. The Zagreb 2025 Worlds were in Arena Zagreb ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Wrestling_Championships)); its mat count is **unsourced**.
- Fits: yes (a circular mat on a platform). Needs the surface. Later.

### Taekwondo

WT Competition Rules and Interpretation, in force 1 June 2026, Article 3: a contest area of 8 by 8 m including a 60 cm boundary line (or an octagon about 8 m across), inside a competition area of "no smaller than 10m x 10m and no larger than 12m x 12m", a safety area round it, an alert band of 60 cm, and the contest area may stand on a platform 0.6 to 1 m high ([WT rules](https://www.taekwondo.dk/files/Dokumenter/Statisk/Dommer/Regler/Competition%20Rules%20and%20Interpretation%20from%2001.06.2026.pdf)). The 2012 rules asked for a hall floor of at least 40 by 60 m with 3,000 seats and 10 m of height, for events the federation hosts ([2012 rules](https://www.mastkd.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/2012-05-23_39660xx_Abr-03_Competition_Rules_Sharm_El-Sheikh_GA.pdf)); it is not a count of areas.

- Real event: the 2025 Worlds, Wuxi, 24 to 30 October 2025, at the Taihu International Expo Center, 991 athletes ([World Taekwondo](https://www.worldtaekwondo.org/wtnews/view.html?nid=142773)). The number of contest areas and their grid are **unsourced**; no event document I found names them.
- Fits: yes, and it would share a mat layout with karate and judo. Needs the surface. Later.

### Snooker and pool

- **Snooker.** The playing area is 11 ft 8.5 in by 5 ft 10 in inside the cushions (WPBSA rules, May 2022; [rulebook](https://wpbsa.com/wp-content/uploads/Rulebook-Website-Updated-May-2022.pdf)). The World Championship qualifiers are played at the English Institute of Sport in Sheffield; a Morning Star article (dated 3 May 2024 on its site) describes "one of two sets of four tables" in a sports hall with temporary seating and a balcony over "the eight tables" ([Morning Star](https://www.morningstaronline.co.uk/node/128108)). The 2013 announcement says spectators could watch several matches at once in the Badminton Hall ([SnookerHQ](https://snookerhq.com/2013/01/21/world-championship-qualifiers-back-in-sheffield/)). The 2026 qualifiers run 6 to 15 April with 128 players, the venue's page says ([Welcome to Sheffield](https://www.welcometosheffield.co.uk/content/events/world-snooker-championship-qualifiers-2026), a page that did not open for me and is read from a search result), but I found no table count for it. The room size or gap between tables is **unsourced** (the only sizes found were furniture-retailer rules of thumb).
- **Pool.** The 9-foot table's playing surface is 100 by 50 inches ([WPA tournament specifications](https://www.poolroom.com/tournament-table-equipment-specifications/)). The 2022 World Pool Championship at the Marshall Arena in Milton Keynes had "17 playing tables and 4 practice tables" ([The Pool Scene](https://thepoolscene.com/posts/diamond-billiards-set-for-2022-world-pool-championship)). Gap: **unsourced**.
- Fits: yes, a table is a flat area with lines (the baulk line, the D, the spots), like the table tennis table. Needs the surface. Later.

### Lawn bowls

A green is "normally square" and 31 to 40 m in the direction of play, divided into rinks "normally six", each 4.3 to 5.8 m wide, with a ditch 200 to 380 mm wide and a bank at least 230 mm high, and the corners of the rinks marked by pegs; the guide cites the Bowls Australia laws ([WA sports guide](https://cits.wa.gov.au/sport-and-recreation/sports-dimensions-guide/bowls---lawn-bowls), secondary; I did not find the World Bowls laws as a file). A green is itself a `1x6` layout of rinks, so it is a surface with sub-areas, not one of several surfaces.

- Real event: the World Bowls Championships on the Gold Coast, 29 August to 10 September 2023, at five clubs: Broadbeach, Musgrave Hill, Helensvale, Paradise Point and Mudgeeraba ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Outdoor_Bowls_Championship), [Bowls Australia](https://www.bowls.com.au/?p=120104)). A search result for the organiser's guide gave "13 premier greens", but I could not open that page: **unverified**.
- Fits: yes. Needs the surface. Later.

### Pétanque

The FIPJP rules (Article 5, in French): a ground holds "an indeterminate number" of lanes marked off with string; the lane for national championships and international competitions is at least 4 m wide and 15 m long, at least 12 by 3 m for other competitions; where a ground is fenced, the barriers stand at least 1 m from the outside line ([FIPJP rules](https://www.fipjp.org/images/pdf/reglementFIPJP.pdf)). The Mondial La Marseillaise à Pétanque, 4 to 9 July 2025 (the 64th), is played at Parc Borély and on "about thirty" other grounds across Marseille, with over 2,000 teams signed up ([Marseille Tourisme](https://www.marseille-tourisme.com/en/vivez-marseille/blog-marseille/mondial-la-marseillaise-a-petanque/), [festivalenfrance](https://www.festivalenfrance.com/festival/mondial-la-marseillaise-a-petanque/2025)). Lanes at Parc Borély: **unsourced**.

- Fits: a lane is flat with a throwing circle, but the rule leaves the number open and a ground has no fixed grid. Later.

### Tenpin bowling

The USBC certification handbook (May 2025) gives a lane length from the foul line to the head pin spot of 59 ft 11.5 in to 60 ft 0.5 in and an approach of at least 15 ft ([USBC](https://bowl.com/getmedia/5d757433-f147-4c1e-b941-1a8e02394a36/May-2025_Tiered-Center-Certification-Handbook_final.pdf)). Lane width is **unsourced** here: the 41.5 in found on reseller pages is a gutter-to-gutter figure and I did not find it in a USBC or World Bowling document. The 2025 IBF World Championships in Hong Kong used the 40-lane Top Bowl at Kai Tak Sports Park, and held the trios and team finals on "2 temporarily installed lanes" in the Queen Elizabeth Stadium in Wan Chai on 4 and 5 December ([The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/317665/HK-hosts-inaugural-IBF-World-Championships-Welcoming-300-Bowlers-from-31-Nations), [ABF](https://abf-online.org/results/ibfwc2025.htm)). Fits: a lane is a flat, lined strip, but a lane is 60 ft by about a metre, so a bank of 40 is a very thin picture. Later.

### Padel

The FIP Rules of Padel (in application from 1 January 2026) give a court of 20 by 10 m inside its walls, at least 6 m of clear height, 8 m suggested for new builds ([FIP rules](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf)). The text names **no gap or free space between courts**. No event with a count was found. Fits: yes, `padel` exists. The footprint of a court with its surround is also **unsourced** here. Wait for a source for the gap.

## Skip, and why

- **Archery.** The range is a shooting line with target lanes 5 m apart (a Western Australian guide, [WA sports guide](https://cits.wa.gov.au/sport-and-recreation/sports-dimensions-guide/archery), secondary; World Archery's Book 2 chapter 7 holds the real figures and is a script-driven site I could not read). A range is 70 to 90 m deep, and the package already draws the kyūdōjō; the interesting picture is the targets, not the surface. No event count found.
- **Darts.** The oche is 2.37 m from the board, the bullseye 1.73 m from the floor ([Lakeside](https://www.lakesideworlddarts.co.uk/?p=25), secondary). The surface is a line on a floor. Pro Tour events use several boards, but I found no hall layout. Nothing to draw.
- **Boxing.** One ring at a time at every event I know of; no multi-ring layout found.
- **Gymnastics.** One hall holds different apparatus, not several of one surface: a different problem from this page. No figures gathered.
- **Chess and esports.** Boards and screens, not a surface with lines.
- **Handball, netball, indoor volleyball.** One court to a hall at the world level; the multi-court halls are training halls and I found no source for a layout. Indoor volleyball's free zones are listed under beach volleyball.
- **Cricket squares.** The pitch is 22 yards by 10 ft, and Law 6 says nothing found about the gap between pitches that lie side by side ([ICC match clause 6, from the MCC Laws](https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/mens-test-match-clause-6-the-pitch)); the number of strips on a square is unsourced.
- **3x3 basketball.** The court is 15 by 11 m ([FIBA 3x3 rules, 2018](https://www.basketball.nl/documents/6/2018_3x3Rules_FIBA.pdf)). Paris 2024 played on one court at the Place de la Concorde; I found no multi-court layout. Revisit with a World Tour source.
