# How it compares

Kyogijo Stadiums is one of several packages and sites that draw playing surfaces or the places they stand in. This page lays them side by side on facts that can be checked: what each draws, for how many sports and leagues, what it knows about parks and grounds, what it does with data, what it needs, and under which licence. It is not a ranking: the others are good at what they are for, several go further than Kyogijo in places (see below the table), and most are older.

How it was made: each project's README was read on 2026-10-13 through the GitHub API (`gh api repos/<owner>/<name>/readme`), with its manifest for the dependencies (`requirements.txt`, `setup.py`, `pyproject.toml`, `DESCRIPTION`) and GitHub's licence field and licence file for the licence. A dash (—) means the README does not describe it: that is not a claim that the project cannot. Only facts were taken, and no code or text of any of them is used here; [references.md](./references.md) says what we may use of each. If a row is wrong or out of date, [open an issue](https://github.com/johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo-stadiums/issues) with the line of the README that says otherwise.

## Where the others go further

- **More leagues.** sportyR and sporty hold 53 league surfaces against Kyogijo's 67 rule sets, 48 of them variants. Kyogijo's are the ones it has a rulebook to cite for (see [League variants](./variants.md)).
- **A wider toolbox.** mplsoccer draws radar, pizza and bumpy charts and loads StatsBomb's open data; the sibling packages of `@sportsdataverse/sporty` do team marks and tables.
- **The data tools beside them.** The R and Python packages sit in the ecosystems where the data is analysed. Kyogijo takes the data you give it and reads no network.
- **Age.** ggsoccer's repository dates from 2017, mplsoccer's from 2019, sportyR's, sportypy's and hockey_rink's from 2021. Kyogijo has been on npm since October 2026 (first release 0.1.0).

## Where Kyogijo goes further

- **More sports.** Pickleball, badminton, table tennis, padel, squash, racquetball, sepak takraw, teqball, roundnet, netball, swimming, water polo, diving, artistic swimming, rugby (union and league), Australian football, Gaelic football and hurling, cricket, field hockey and handball, none of which any of the three lists; lacrosse, which sportyR and `@sportsdataverse/sporty` draw too, is here from four governing bodies' rulebooks, the field and the box.
- **A surface is also a scoreboard.** The teams' names and logos on their sides and the score in the middle, wide or tall, or as a strip for a list, in a drawing that says all of it to a screen reader.
- **Places, not only surfaces.** Ballparks of today and of the past, each with its own fence, soccer grounds at their published pitch sizes, each with its Wikidata item, and an atlas of them all.
- **Time.** A surface as the rules marked it in a season, each change cited.
- **A surface inside another.** An NHL rink on a ballpark's infield or a football stadium's field, a basketball court on a carrier's deck, a baseball field laid over a stadium's pitch: to scale, each with its sources, and marked as a convention where no source says where the surface stood.
- **Looks and labels that hold.** Seven named looks whose every line is tested for contrast in light and dark, and labels, axes and arrows that stay upright when a surface is turned.
- **No dependencies and no runtime but the browser's.** A drawing is a string from a pure function, the same in Node, an edge function and a page.
- **Renderers to match, and two more.** The drawing is plain data (a scene) that paints to a canvas, a d3 selection and an Observable Plot as `@sportsdataverse/sporty` does, and also to GeoJSON, in feet or a provider's frame, and to a PNG, with no dependency in the core and the one optional peer for the PNG ([Renderers](./renderers.md)).
- **Small multiples and grounds in one picture.** A grid of drawings or charts with a title, a key and a credit line, one SVG with a group title and a title for each drawing, in the size of a social card or a phone; and a pattern or a photograph behind any drawing or grid ([Layouts and backgrounds](./layouts.md)).
- **English and Japanese** in the drawing's words, its statuses and the sentence for each change.

## What each README says

Each cell above is from the README or the manifest named here; where a README gives a count, the count is quoted.

- **[sportypy](https://github.com/sportsdataverse/sportypy)** (Python, Matplotlib): surfaces for baseball, basketball, curling, football, hockey, soccer and tennis; a `display_range` that zooms to a red zone or a house (the README's example is the NFL's red zone); `x_trans`, `y_trans` and `rotation`; a table of the rulebook unit of 44 leagues (volleyball included); `scatter`, `arrow`, `heatmap` and `contourf` on a surface; requirements `matplotlib`, `numpy`, `pandas`, `scipy`; GPL-3.0.
- **[sportyR](https://github.com/sportsdataverse/sportyR)** (R, ggplot2): the same for R, with lacrosse, softball and volleyball too (53 leagues in its units table); `DESCRIPTION` imports ggfittext, ggplot2, glue, grid and rlang; GPL (>= 3).
- **[@sportsdataverse/sporty](https://github.com/sportsdataverse/sdvplot-js/tree/main/packages/sporty)** (TypeScript): a port of sportyR 2.2.3, nine sports and 53 league surfaces as a scene that draws to SVG, canvas, Observable Plot or d3; `toSurfaceFrame` with four frames; basketball shot zones; units that convert the anchors too; no dependency for the core; MIT. The repository's other packages, [sdvplot and sdvtables](https://github.com/sportsdataverse/sdvplot-js), do team marks (a mark picked by season) and tables.
- **[hockey_rink](https://github.com/the-bucketless/hockey_rink)** (Python, Matplotlib): hockey rinks, with notebooks for drawing (a dark-theme rink included), plotting (wrappers for Matplotlib's methods, binned plots shown) and animating; requirements `matplotlib`, `numpy`, `pillow`; GPL-3.0.
- **[mplsoccer](https://github.com/andrewRowlinson/mplsoccer)** (Python, Matplotlib): "nine different pitch types", radar, Nightingale or pizza and bumpy charts (its docs gallery, read the same day, also has the pages Pass Network, Pass flow plot, Convex Hull, Plots Delaunay Tessellation of Players, Plots a Voronoi diagram, Formations and the sonars Sonar, Sonar Grid, Sonar Zones and Bin Statistic Sonar), arrows, heatmaps, hexbins, scatter and comet lines, a loader for StatsBomb's open data, a `grid` of pitches with a title and an endnote (read in `mplsoccer/grid.py`, not the README), a standardiser of pitch coordinates; its docs gallery has an animation page; MIT.
- **[ggsoccer](https://github.com/Torvaney/ggsoccer)** (R, ggplot2): pitches as ggplot layers; Opta (the default), StatsBomb, Wyscout and Tracab out of the box, any custom specification, `rescale_coordinates`, goal styles, a direction label, colour, fill, line width, line type and alpha, a custom goal function; `DESCRIPTION` imports ggplot2; MIT + file LICENSE.
- **[GeomMLBStadiums](https://github.com/bdilday/GeomMLBStadiums)** (R, ggplot2): “the 30 current MLB stadiums, in addition to a ‘generic’ stadium” as paths in MLBAM's `hc_x` and `hc_y`, with `mlbam_xy_transformation` and a spray-chart geom; imports ggplot2 and grid; MIT + file LICENSE.
- **[mlb-spring-training-stadiums](https://github.com/gavinr/mlb-spring-training-stadiums)** (data): the Cactus League (10 parks) and Grapefruit League (13) as CSV (name, latitude, longitude, teams, address) and GeoJSON, "originally obtained from Wikipedia in March 2013", with an ArcGIS map; no licence file; "Everyone is welcome to contribute or use this data."
- **[thestadi.com](https://thestadi.com)** (a website): "an interactive map showing all NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL, and MLS stadiums and arenas across North America" (32, 30, 30, 32 and 30 teams), with search, a filter by league, team logos and venue details.
