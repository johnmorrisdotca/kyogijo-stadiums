## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **The pools in the 3D view, at the heights of World Aquatics' rules** ([docs/turn.md](./docs/turn.md#what-stands-on-each-surface), [docs/sources.md](./docs/sources.md#heights-in-the-3d-view)). A swimming pool has a numbered starting block 0.7 m high in each lane at each end, a flagged rope 1.8 m above the water 5 m from each wall and the false start rope 1.2 m above it at 15 m; a diving well its springboards (1 m and 3 m) and platforms (5, 7.5 and 10 m, or the ones asked for) on stands and towers; a water polo field a floating goal on each goal line, 3 m wide with the bar's underside 0.9 m over the water; an artistic swimming area the judges' platform (0.6 m) and the starting platform (0.7 m). New upright kinds `starting-blocks`, `backstroke-flags`, `false-start-rope`, `springboards`, `platforms` and `judges-stand`, in English and Japanese (スタート台, 背泳ぎ用フラッグ, フォルススタートロープ, 飛板, 固定台, ジャッジ台). The pools are in the e2e list of surfaces that stand up.
- **Walls give way to the eye in the 3D view.** The glass and walls of a padel court, the walls of squash and racquetball courts and the boards and glass of a rink are cut away on the side nearest the eye, more the lower the eye is, and are whole again from above or from the far side: `Plane.shy`, `fadeOf` and `Layout.fade` (`/turn`).
- **A folded curling sheet has no crowd.** Its enlarged houses cover where the stands would be, so `crowd` on a folded sheet draws nothing; the same sheet with a score shows the whole sheet and keeps its stands, with a pageant as well.
- **A folded curling sheet: `sheet.show: "folded"`** ([docs/curling.md](./docs/curling.md#the-top-end-the-bottom-end-or-both-folded)). The whole sheet as a strip, with its two houses enlarged beside it, so that a sheet 150 feet long and 15 wide fills a roughly square frame. A tall drawing has the top house on the left of the strip and the bottom house on the right; a wide one has the left house above and the right house below (the same drawing turned a quarter). The strip keeps its scale and its numbers, so a data layer lies on it as on a sheet drawn whole; each panel is the same sheet from the hog line to the back line (`sheet.panels: "half"`, the default) or just the house (`"house"`), enlarged until the whole has the shape `sheet.fit` asks (width over height, 0.5 to 2, a square by default; a panel is never longer than the strip), labelled ("Top house", "Bottom house", "Left house", "Right house"; in Japanese too) and joined to the part of the strip it enlarges by a faint fan and a frame. Stones, heat and every other layer are drawn in the strip and again in each panel, at the panel's scale, with the copies hidden from a screen reader and ids of their own. `range: "folded"` says the same (a new name in `RANGES.curling`), the description says what is enlarged and where, a score still shows the whole sheet, and the annotations (`landmarks`, `axes`, `direction`) are not drawn on it. The scene, the canvas, d3, Plot, GeoJSON and PNG read it like any drawing. New: `SheetShow` has `"folded"`, `SheetStyle.panels` and `SheetStyle.fit`, `RangeName` has `"folded"`, and `KYOGIJO_FOLD_STYLE` (written in only by a folded sheet; every other curling drawing is the same text as before).
- **`top` and `bottom` as names for a tall sheet's ends.** `sheet.end` and `range: { name, end }` take `top` (the left end, the far house of a tall drawing, the default) and `bottom` (the right end) as well as `left` and `right`; the new type is `SheetEnd`. The top end alone and the bottom end alone are `show: "half"` or `"house"` with either, and are documented as the three ways to look closer at a tall sheet.
- **`curlingHeat`: where the stones of many ends came to rest** (`@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo-stadiums/charts`, [docs/curling.md](./docs/curling.md#where-the-stones-of-many-ends-came-to-rest)). A chart on the heat layers: stones counted into square cells (a foot by default), hexagons, a density or the zones of the house (the button, the three rings and the free guard zone), or drawn as translucent discs in each team's colour so that stones that fall on each other build up colour. It judges the stones by `curlingScore`'s rules, and keeps one team's stones (`team`), only the free guards or only the stones in the house (`only`); on the half, the house, the whole sheet or the folded sheet (`show`). The totals say how many stones were in play and drawn, the free guards and the stones in the house for each team, and the share in each zone. New: `CurlingHeatOptions`, `CurlingHeatTotals`, the `titleCurlingHeat`, `curlingHeatStat`, `sumCurlingHeat`, `curlingHeatTeam`, `curlingHeatGuards` and `curlingHeatHouse` words of `KYOGIJO_STRINGS[...].charts` (`en` and `ja`; a custom set of words must give them) and `CurlingOptions.show`, so that `curlingStones` can be folded too.
- **Curling statistics, beside `curlingScore`:** `curlingFreeGuards` (the free guards of a layout: a stone in play between the tee line and the hog line and not touching the house), `curlingInPlay` (the stones in play after each throw, and the most), `curlingHammers` and `curlingEndStats` (each end's record, and for each team its points, hammer efficiency, how often it scored, blanked, was held to one or was stolen from with the hammer, and its steal rate without it), `curlingGuardScores` (the free guard analysis: points against guards, by band, for the team with the hammer and the team without it) and `curlingShotStats` with `curlingIsHit` and `CURLING_HIT_TYPES` (shot percentage, hit rate and the types played). Pure functions of plain data. Written from the rules and from what curlovision and Jordan Myslik's curling analytics describe; nothing of either was copied (see [references](./docs/references.md#curling)), and [Curling](./docs/curling.md#what-other-curling-analyses-offer-and-what-is-here) compares what each offers with what is here.
- **`HeatLegend.region`** (and the same on a `legend` layer): a heat layer's colour bar is sized and placed against a part of the surface in feet instead of the whole of what is shown, so that it can be read on the half of a sheet or in an enlarged house.
- The docs' curling gallery has the folded sheet, the top end and the bottom end, the heat chart in each of its views and a game's ends in one picture; the demo has a Show choice for a curling sheet (whole sheet, top end, bottom end, folded) with finger-wide buttons, in English and Japanese, in the address and in the code it writes.
- **A realistic basketball floor** (`court: { hardwood: true }`, [docs/hardwood.md](./docs/hardwood.md)). The court seen from above as a maple strip floor: boards of one width (2.25 inches by default) running the length of the court, each row ending in different places from the rows next to it, each board with a tone of its own and fine grain along it, all laid from a seed (`seed`, 1 by default) with a generator of our own, so the same options give the same floor in every renderer; the keys, the centre circle and the border are stain, with the wood and the seams showing through (`stain`, 0.82 by default); the strip behind each baseline takes the colour of the team that defends it (`leftColor` and `rightColor`, a team's `color` in a score, or the new `baselineColor`); the baseline words are set in that strip and new `sidelineText` along the sides; `centreLogo` (an image or an SVG document) fills the centre circle; each basket is drawn from above with its backboard, rim, net and padded stanchion and a soft shadow; the coaching box's lines, the scorers' table and the two benches stand along the bottom sideline; and a satin sheen and the shade at the floor's edge are gradients over the paint (`sheen`). Every league of the sport takes it (NBA, WNBA, FIBA, NCAA men's and women's, NFHS, G League), wide and tall, in light and dark, with a score, a `range`, a `host`, and the data layers and charts over it. The detail drops at small sizes: `detail: "auto"` draws the plain floor (broad boards, the stain and the sheen, one shadow, no seams, grain, table or benches) for `detail: "simple"` or a `width` under 480 pixels, and `fine` or `plain` can be asked for. It is made only of patterns, gradients and rectangles, so the scene, the canvas, d3, Plot, GeoJSON and PNG renderers all draw it: the SVG is about 30 kB in the fine detail (the clean court's is about 11 kB) and about 15 kB in the plain, from two tiles of pattern and about 90 more shapes. Off by default, and a drawing without it is the same text as before. New: the options `HardwoodOptions` and `CourtStyle.hardwood`, `CourtStyle.baselineColor` and `CourtStyle.sidelineText`, `KYOGIJO_HARDWOOD_STYLE` (also in `KYOGIJO_STYLE`) and `HARDWOOD_SMALL`.
- The demo's court controls have a "Real hardwood" chip (a finger wide on a phone, in English and Japanese), and the court cards show the real floor in a club's colours and its plain version for small sizes; the docs gallery has the floor in nine drawings, each made from the code beside it.

The clubs and their leagues are Wikidata's (CC0, the places' occupants P466 and the clubs' home venues P115) and the `tenants` lines of the infoboxes of the places' English Wikipedia articles (CC BY-SA 4.0, at the revision named); the years are Wikidata's where it has them and the article's otherwise, never guessed. `pnpm tenants:fetch` reads them and `pnpm tenants` builds `src/tenants.data.ts`.

New: `TENANTS`, the types `Tenant`, `TenantArticle`, `TenantSource`, `TenantSport`, `TenantFilter`, `PlaceSurface` and `SurfaceSport`.

- **A 3D view: `@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo-stadiums/turn`** ([docs/turn.md](./docs/turn.md)). Any surface as a floor that turns: the flat drawing itself (with its score, data layers and labels) tipped and turned by the mouse, a finger, Ctrl and the wheel or the keys, with four viewpoints (`overhead`, `broadcast`, `end`, `corner`; the broadcast view is turned a little to show the boards, nets and goals their faces, the net sports' is from behind an end line, where a net can be seen, and a curling sheet's is square on) and a reset, in plain DOM and CSS with no dependency. `mountKyogijoTurn(container, options, settings)` mounts it and gives back `view()`, `set()`, `preset()`, `reset()`, `update()`, `svg()` and `destroy()`; `<kyogijo-field turn turn-view="end">` is the element's way (`turn-controls`, `turn-uprights`, `turn-touch`, and `turn` and `turnSettings` properties); `turnMarkup(options, settings)` is the same view as a string, pure, for a server. Every part that stands up is a flat rectangle with a little SVG on it, and every element carries its whole place on the screen in one `matrix3d`, stacked far to near by `z-index`: nothing is nested in 3D (no `preserve-3d`), so Safari on a phone cannot flatten it, and the flat drawing stays where 3D is not available (`turnSupported`), with a sentence that says so. What stands on each surface, at the height the current rulebook gives (each read on 2026-10-13 and cited by rule number in [docs/sources.md](./docs/sources.md#heights-in-the-3d-view), with what is ours said to be): the NHL's glass is 8 ft over the boards at the ends and 6 ft along the sides, the NFL's uprights 45 ft, the CFL's 40 ft and the NCAA's at least 30 ft, rugby league's 16 m, a basket's board lower edge 150 mm under the ring (FIBA 1.1.5) and its stanchion 2 m behind the end line (FIBA 1.4.3).  the volleyball net at 2.43 m or 2.24 m (`netHeight: "women"`) with its posts and antennae, the nets and posts of tennis (and singles sticks), badminton, pickleball and table tennis (the table lifted 76 cm on legs), each basket's backboard, rim, net and stanchion, a rink's boards, glass and goals, the goals of soccer (with corner flags), handball, field hockey and lacrosse, the posts and pylons of football, the posts of rugby, Gaelic football and Australian football, a cricket pitch's stumps, and a ballpark's fence (as high as its walls), foul poles and backstop. `uprightsFor` and `describeUprights` give them as planes and as a sentence; the view's description says what stands in it, with its heights in the rulebook's own unit, in English and Japanese. A finger drags sideways to turn the view and leaves a vertical swipe to the page (`touch: "turn"` keeps every touch); the wheel is the page's unless Ctrl is held or the view is focused; the keys are the arrows, plus and minus, 1 to 4 and 0; the buttons are 44 pixels or more; a viewpoint, a reset and the end of a drag are said in a polite live region; with `prefers-reduced-motion` the view jumps instead of gliding. New: `mountKyogijoTurn`, `turnMarkup`, `turnMarkupOf`, `buildTurn`, `layoutOf`, `turnSupported`, `uprightsFor`, `describeUprights`, `planesOf`, `heightUnit`, `TURN_PRESETS`, `NET_PRESETS`, `TURN_PRESET_NAMES`, `KYOGIJO_TURN_STYLE`, the camera's `fit`, `project`, `cameraMatrix`, `clampView`, `drag` and `step`, the types `KyogijoTurn`, `TurnSettings`, `TurnView`, `TurnPreset`, `TurnModel`, `Uprights`, `UprightOptions`, `Plane` and the rest, the `turn` words of `KYOGIJO_STRINGS` (`en` and `ja`; a custom set of words must give them), and `pitchOf` is exported from the drawing's own module for it.

- **Seven more court sports, and sitting volleyball** ([docs/courts.md](./docs/courts.md)), each drawn to scale from its governing body's own current rules, with its named looks (light and dark), a free zone where the rulebook gives one, simple and full detail, wide and tall, an outline, a description and labelled parts in English and Japanese, a frame, ranges, a score and a badge (`SPORTS` is now 27):
  - **`padel`** (the FIP's Rules of Padel, review of 1 January 2026): 20 by 10 m inside its walls, the service lines at 6.95 m, the central service line, the net, and the walls and metal fence of the enclosure.
  - **`squash`** (World Squash's Specifications for Squash Courts, 28 November 2017): the short line, half court line and service boxes of the floor, with the front wall laid flat beside it to show its tin, service line and front wall line; `league: "wsf-doubles"` and `"wsf-doubles-wide"` for the doubles courts.
  - **`racquetball`** (the IRF's Official Rules 2026-2028 and Court Specifications): 40 by 20 feet, the short line, service line, service boxes, drive serve lines and the broken receiving line.
  - **`sepaktakraw`** (the ISTAF's Law of the Game, 2013 edition, the one that could be read): 13.4 by 6.1 m, the centre line, four quarter circles, two service circles, the net.
  - **`teqball`** (the FITEQ's rules, 2026): the 3 by 1.5 m table and its net in the middle of a court of 16 by 12 m, the halfway line, the service lines, the surrounds.
  - **`roundnet`** (the International Roundnet Federation's rules, 2022): the set, the no hit zone line, the serving line and the four service positions, on a square with no out of bounds.
  - **`netball`** (World Netball's Rules of Netball, 2024): 30.5 by 15.25 m, the transverse lines, centre circle, goal circles and goalposts with their rings; scored as a goals sport, with the usual score and the home team on the left.
  - **`league: "wpv-sitting"`** of `volleyball` (World ParaVolley's Official Sitting Volleyball Rules 2025-2028): 10 by 6 m, the attack line at 2 m, a 7 m net 1.15 m high.
  - New zone sets `wall-court` (squash, racquetball) and `roundnet-rings`; `net-court` now covers padel, sepak takraw and teqball, and `field-thirds` netball. 36 coordinate frames (7 more), 7 more `half-court` ranges, and 7 more sports in the demo's picker, its gallery and the docs gallery, with the sources and licence notes in [docs/sources.md](./docs/sources.md) and [docs/references.md](./docs/references.md).
  - The colours follow the rulebooks where they give one: squash's lines are red (a dark red on the light floor, a pale red on the dark one; World Squash's specification asks only that markings contrast, clauses 5.01.01 and 10.13, so the red is ours) on the floor and the front wall, with the wall's lines drawn separately as the specification allows; racquetball's lines are the IRF's recommended bright red (blue on the convertible look) on a floor light enough to read them; padel's, netball's and sitting volleyball's lines are white as their rules prefer or require, and sitting volleyball has the wooden floor only (a light indoor floor, Rule 1.2.2).
  - Footvolley is not drawn: the only rulebook found that gives its dimensions is a European league's, not an international governing body's. Futsal is `pitch: "futsal"` of `soccer`, as before.
- **The stands and the crowd** (`crowd: true`, [docs/crowd.md](./docs/crowd.md)) on every surface that has spectators. Seating bowls drawn round the surface at the distance a venue of its kind keeps: a bowl, two sides (`sides`), a horseshoe open at an end (`horseshoe`, `open`), an oval round the oval grounds of Australian football and cricket, an arena's courtside rows with a lower and an upper bowl for the rinks and courts, and for a ballpark grandstands along the foul lines and behind home plate with bleachers behind the park's own fence (laid from `fenceOutline`, with a gap at the batter's eye), each stand on a plaza of its own. A soccer pitch in the stadium of an NFL or CFL club (`GRIDIRON_GROUNDS`, or `stadium: "nfl"` or `"cfl"`) keeps the football field's footprint: the stands stand where a football field's stands are and the 360 by 160 foot field is drawn faintly over the pitch (end zones, five-yard lines, dashed outline), so that the gap is seen to scale: the touchlines are 8.5 feet from the front rows, not the 28 to 36 of a ground built for soccer. The people are a seeded tile of 36 seats repeated as an SVG pattern, each seat empty with the probability the density leaves or a dot of shirt (the team's colour in two shades, or a neutral) with a dot of hair over it, so that the stands are a few shapes and a few kB (about 9 kB added to a soccer drawing, 4.5 kB as blocks) however many seats there are: `density` (0 to 1, 0.85 by default), or `attendance` over `capacity` (a ground's own capacity by default for soccer), `seed`, `home` and `away` colours (the teams' colours of a score, else the sides'), the visitors' block at an end or in a corner (`awayEnd`), a card display in stripes or checks behind a goal (`tifo`, `tifoDesign`), and a tunnel, a camera platform, benches and a media row on the bench side (`gaps`). Aisles and the shade of each tier's front wall and roof edge are drawn in the `fine` detail; for `detail: "simple"` or a `width` under 480 pixels (`detail: "auto"`) the stands are blocks of colour with no pattern. The drawing's ground is made as wide as the stands need (a `margin` of your own is kept). It works wide and tall, light and dark (the crowd is dimmer in the dark), as a score with its round marks, with `range` (a crop shows the stands it covers) and with the data layers over it; a special game (`host`) has none. The scene, the canvas, d3, Plot and PNG renderers draw it. `crowdStands(options)` gives the stands as data in feet in the surface's wide frame (rings with their outlines, `from` and `to`, the height of the first and last row, the rake in degrees, the seats; the visitors' and the card display's sectors; the gaps; the footprint; the reach), with `outlinePath` and `outlinePoints`, so that a view that raises the stands (the 3D view of `work/turn`) can use it: a follow-up. Off by default, and a drawing without it is the same text as before. New: `CrowdOptions`, `CrowdShape`, `CrowdStands`, `StandRing`, `StandSector`, `StandGap`, `StandOutline`, `GRIDIRON_GROUNDS` and `KYOGIJO_CROWD_STYLE` (also in `KYOGIJO_STYLE`; `--ky-cr-home` and `--ky-cr-away` are the crowd's colours).
- **The stands read as seats from above** (polish of the crowd). Row lines every second row follow each tier's curves, with aisles across every tier (square to the front on the sides, fanning round the corners) and every fourth a wider dark vomitory; the visitors' block and a card display are whole sections cut on aisle lines, not wedges (a wedge only round an oval ground); the walk between the tiers is a thin darker band (`concourses`) instead of a wide pale ring, and the roof's edge throws a shade on the upper tier (`roof`, on by default). A soccer pitch's run-off is the 6 metres along the touchlines and 8.5 behind the goal lines that stadium guidance recommends (19.7 and 27.9 feet, 27.7 on the bench side), so that a pitch in a gridiron stadium has the narrower apron at the touchlines (8.5 feet) and a wider one behind the goals (33.8). A ballpark's grandstand edge is a smooth curve with no notches, its bleachers stand 12 feet behind the fence, and where the park has seats on top of a wall (Fenway Park's Green Monster) a band of them runs along the wall at its height (`wallSeats`, `WALL_SEATS`). The stands data gains `aisles` on every ring and `concourses`, a wall's seats are a ring with the tier `wall`, and the tiers of an arena are 7 feet apart.

- **The pageant: each team's flag or crest laid large over its half of the surface, and an emblem in the centre** ([docs/pageant.md](./docs/pageant.md)). `pageant: "carpet"`, `"stage"`, `true` (a carpet) or `PageantOptions` on a score drawing, off by default, so a drawing without it is the same text as before. A **carpet** is the flag or logo laid flat over its half, filling the room the half gives it with a margin of about 5% (`fit: "fill"`, the default: its shape may differ from the flag's ratio by up to 1.75 to 1, the art stretched a little and cropped a little; `fit: "ratio"` keeps the flag's own ratio, `Team.logoRatio`, else the `viewBox` of an SVG logo, else 3 to 2), with a border band (`border`: `dark`, `team` or a colour; `borderWidth`), a soft shadow and a gentle fixed wave of light and shade (`fabric`); a **stage** is the crest on a large round disc with a ring (`ring`: `gold`, `team` or a colour), the crest a round mark inside it. A team with no flag or logo is its colours as a carpet of two with its name across a band, or its initials on its colour on a stage. `emblem` (an address, an image `data:` address or an SVG document as text: yours, none is supplied; `emblemShape` `round` or `plain`, `emblemFit`, `emblemName`) goes in the centre circle, centre ice, centre court, the middle of a field or sheet, or on the mound. Every surface has a place for the pictures: the halves, each side of a cricket pitch, a diamond's infield (home) and outfield (visitors), a curling sheet's two houses; each lies wholly inside the surface's outline (a rink's rounded corners, a fence, an oval). Tall and wide drawings lay the halves where the reader sees them and keep every picture upright. The score stays readable by `score`: `centre` (the plate stays, 15% smaller, and the pictures keep clear of it), `strip` (the compact strip under the drawing, carrying each team's round mark) or `auto` (the default: the strip with an emblem or a large board, else whichever leaves the pictures at least 70% of their width). `lines` is `over` (the default), `under` or `through` the pictures; `scale` and `names` size and name them; under 320 pixels wide or with `detail: "simple"` the fabric, hairlines and name pills are left out; dark theme has its own colours (`--ky-pg-border`, `--ky-pg-edge`, `--ky-pg-seam`, `--ky-pg-shadow`, `--ky-pg-disc`, `--ky-pg-groove`, `--ky-pg-light`); a `range` shows the carpet where the range shows it. `animate: "unfurl"` or `"fade"` is an entrance in CSS only, off by default, inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`. Every renderer draws it (the scene, canvas, d3, Plot, PNG); `sceneToGeoJSON` leaves it out, since the GeoJSON is of the surface. New: `Surface.pageant` and `centreRadius` (where a builder puts the pictures and its centre circle), `Team.logoRatio`, the `pageant` words of `KYOGIJO_STRINGS` (`en` and `ja`; a custom set of words must give them), `KYOGIJO_PAGEANT_STYLE`, `KYOGIJO_PAGEANT_MOTION`, the types `PageantOptions` and `PageantStyle`, the `pageant` attribute of `<kyogijo-field>`, and the root's `data-pageant`.

One call each makes the layers and the drawing options for a chart, so a good chart takes one line and every layer can still be changed: `nbaShotChart` (made and missed shots and the six shot zones coloured by field-goal percentage, diverging from the league's), `nhlShotMap` (goals and shots sized by expected goals, `fold` to put both ends on one, slot zones or hexagons), `mlbSprayChart` (balls in play on the park of their season, past parks included, a mark for each hit type, balls beyond the fence drawn beyond it, spray zones by the side the batter bats from, Retrosheet codes as well as Statcast's `hc_x` and `hc_y`), `nflPassChart` (destinations by outcome and air yards, the line of scrimmage, the pass grid's completion percentages) and `soccerShotMap` with `goalMouth()` / `soccerGoalMouth` (shots sized by expected goals on the pitch or its half, a view of the goal frame with where each shot crossed the goal line, xG totals). Each chart has a key, a legend, a title and description for every layer and a table; the drawings are deterministic, held by fingerprints and size budgets; its words are in English and Japanese; every example on the page is run by a test. The tables of layers and heat layers now name the unit of a frame's own numbers (tenths of a foot for the NBA's) and not the drawing's. The demo has a Charts section.
- **Heat layers.** Five new kinds of data layer: `heatmap` (a binned statistic on a grid over the surface), `hexbin`, `zones` (named zones with aggregated values and labels), `contour` (a Gaussian kernel density, Scott's rule by default, drawn as filled bands or lines) and `legend`. Statistics are `count`, `sum`, `mean`, `median`, `max`, `min`, `percent`, `rate` (a field-goal percentage from made and missed) or a function, with `normalize`, `minCount`, a `domain`, a diverging `center` and cell or zone labels. Cells, hexagons and bands are cut to the surface's outline. Ten zone sets, each cited in docs/sources.md: soccer positional zones (juego de posición), thirds, channels and the 18 zones; the NBA's shot zones; the NHL's slot and home plate; baseball's spray zones and Retrosheet's sectors; football's pass grid. Five built-in colour ramps (`ember`, `ocean`, `mono`, `balance`, `tide`) with a light and a dark set, checked against every surface's colour; custom stops. Each layer has a title, a description that names its hottest cells or zones and, with `layerTable(layers, { surface })`, a table of its numbers. A 50 by 30 grid is held under 30 kB on every sport and 10,000 marks make a density in under 500 ms. The `/layers` entry exports the numbers (`binGrid`, `hexBins`, `densityGrid`, `contourRings` and more), `ZONE_SETS` and `buildZoneSet`. The demo's Data section shows a rink heat map, an NBA shot-zone chart with field-goal percentages, a soccer positional heat map and a density contour.
- **Past ballparks.** `PARKS` grows by 44 past parks, and every park carries its Wikidata item (`wikidata`, `parkByWikidata`). A past park has the seasons it was used (`from`, `to`) and, where an article dates earlier fences, `periods`, chosen by the new `year` option (`parkPointsIn`).
- **Units.** `SURFACE_UNITS` names the unit each rulebook states its figures in; `convertLength`, `lengthsIn`, `dimensionsOf`, `surfaceSize` and `fenceIn` give every dimension in feet, inches, yards, metres, centimetres or millimetres; the `units` option says what the lengths of a drawing's options (`margin`, `fence`) are in.
- **Coordinate frames** (`@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo/frames`). `toSurface`, `fromSurface` and `convert` put a provider's points on a surface and move them between providers, with `units`, `anchors`, `limits` and `attacking`; the frames of the NHL API, HockeyTech, NBA stats, NFL tracking data, MLBAM and Retrosheet's hit locations; `registerFrame` and `piecewise` for the frames of other sports.
- **Soccer.** `sport: "soccer"` draws a pitch from Law 1 of the IFAB Laws of the Game at any size (`pitch`), with presets (international, UEFA, MLS, NCAA, the Laws' least and most, the FA's 9-, 7- and 5-a-side, FIFA's futsal), half pitches, six mowing patterns, four goal styles, corner flags, teams in their penalty areas and a score with extra time and penalties (`Team.penalties`, `final-aet`, `final-pens`). - **Data layers** (`layers` in the options; `drawLayers`, `layerTable` and the `@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo/layers` entry). Points (seven shapes and team logos), lines, arrows (curved, wavy, open or filled heads, comet lines that fade along their length) and text over any surface, in the surface's feet, any unit or a provider's frame, cut to the boards, the fence or the court's lines with a clip path for each drawing, upright when the surface lies tall. Size by value, colour by value, team or outcome, filled and hollow outcomes (`OUTCOMES`), new colour properties (`--ky-layer`, `--ky-layer-home`, `--ky-layer-away`, `--ky-layer-edge`, `--ky-layer-hollow`, `--ky-layer-ink`, `--ky-layer-halo`) that follow a score drawing's teams. A title and a description for each layer, a tooltip for each mark and an HTML table of the data. 1,000 points add about 60 kB; 10,000 are drawn in about 20 ms. The demo has a Data section with a shot map, a pass map and a spray chart made from seeded, made-up data.
- **Surfaces through the years** (`@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo/eras`). `year` draws a surface as its league's rules marked it in a season, for the NHL, the NBA, the NFL, the CFL and baseball: the NHL's centre red line, goal lines, blue lines and trapezoid, the NBA's lane, three-point line and restricted area, the NFL's hash marks and goal posts, the CFL's end zones, hash marks and goal posts (and the field planned for 2027), the baseball pitcher's box and plate, batter's boxes and home plate. `rulesFor`, `eraChanges`, `eraStates`, `eraRange`, `seasonLabel` and `footballIn`; each change has the season it began in, a sentence in English and Japanese and its sources, and a marking no source covers is listed in `gaps` rather than guessed. ### Changed

- **Breaking: the park status `"historic"` is now `"past"`.** `Park["status"]` is `"current" | "secondary" | "past"`, as for the grounds and the atlas; the six NPB parks that read `"historic"` read `"past"` (the type and the value in `PARKS`). Code that compares `park.status === "historic"` must compare with `"past"`.
- **Breaking: `Sport` and `SPORTS` grow from 6 to 20 sports.** The type was `"nfl" | "cfl" | "ifaf" | "nhl" | "nba" | "mlb"`; it now also has `"soccer"`, `"curling"`, the net sports (`"tennis"`, `"volleyball"`, `"badminton"`, `"pickleball"`, `"tabletennis"`) and the field sports (`"lacrosse"`, `"rugby"`, `"afl"`, `"gaelic"`, `"cricket"`, `"fieldhockey"`, `"handball"`). A TypeScript `switch` over `Sport` that is checked to be exhaustive must add a case for each, and code that loops over `SPORTS` now meets all twenty.
- **Breaking: the NHL trapezoid is 22 feet wide at the goal line, not 18.** `NHL.trapezoid.atGoalLine` was 18 feet; Rule 1.8 has 22 feet, the figure since 2014-15, when the trapezoid was widened by 2 feet each side (18 feet from 2005-06 to 2013-14, which `rulesFor("nhl", year)` and a drawing with `year` still give). The rink is drawn with the wider trapezoid by default.
- **Breaking: the NHL's hash marks are 5 feet 7 inches apart, not 5 feet 11 inches.** The marks beside an end-zone circle were drawn 5 feet 11 inches apart, which added the lines' width to a gap it does not enter; Rule 1.9 says 5 feet 7 inches (`NHL.hashApart`). The AHL, ECHL, OHL, WHL and PWHL, which share the NHL's Rule 1, change with it. docs/sources.md also records where the NHL's Rule 1.8 (a 22-foot trapezoid at the goal line, drawn) and Rule 27.8 (18 feet) disagree.
- **Breaking: MLB bases are 18 inches square, not 15.** `MLB.baseSize` was 15 inches; Rule 2.03 of the Official Baseball Rules has 18 inches from the 2023 season. The size is now a marking in `rulesFor("mlb", year)` (`baseSize`, 15 inches before 2023, 18 from 2023, with `eraChanges("mlb")` listing it), so the field is drawn with 18-inch bases by default and 15-inch ones for a season before 2023.
- **Breaking: the SVG written by `drawKyogijo` changes.** Every NHL drawing (the trapezoid and the hash marks) and every MLB drawing (the bases) differs from 0.1.0's, so a stored copy of an old drawing, or a snapshot test of one, needs to be made again. Every drawing's stylesheet is also about 2 kB larger.

### Fixed

- **NHL hash marks were 3 feet apart before 2014-15.** The rulebook of 2012-13 (Rule 1.9) says "three feet (3') apart", and the NHL and NHLPA release of 6 October 2014 moves them to 5 feet 7 inches for 2014-15. `rulesFor("nhl", year).rules.hashApart` is 3 feet through 2013-14 and 5 feet 7 inches after, `eraChanges("nhl")` lists both (`nhl.hashApart.2012`, `nhl.hashApart.2014`) and a rink drawn for an earlier season has the marks 3 feet apart. Seasons before 2012-13 are cited by no rulebook read, so they keep 3 feet and `gaps` names `hashApart`. The AHL, ECHL, OHL, WHL and PWHL are unchanged: no page read dates a change of their marks.
- **docs/sources.md cited the wrong baseball rule for home plate.** Home plate is Official Baseball Rules 2.02 (Home Base) in the 2024 edition; 2.03 is The Bases. The 90-foot square is 2.01, not 2.01 and 2.02.
