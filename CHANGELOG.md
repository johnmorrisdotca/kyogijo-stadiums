## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **`roadToFinal`: the road to a tournament's final** (`@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo/charts`, [docs/charts.md](./docs/charts.md#the-road-to-the-final)). The two finalists as large round marks with their names; down the middle the rounds, the last at the top, on a dotted road; on each finalist's side its matches, each as the round mark of both teams with the score between them in large type and FT, AET or the shoot-out under it; and the group stage as a row of small marks, three-letter codes and scores at the foot. A finalist is a `Team` with a `path` of `{ round, opponent, score, decidedBy, pens, homeFirst, note }` and an optional `group`; a round is a key with words in English and Japanese (`round-of-32` to `semi-final`) or any key named in `labels`, so a play-off series (`4-2`) draws as well as a knockout. `final` writes the final's score between the large marks and quiets the loser; `winner` makes the side that won each match brighter; `codes`, `endnote`, `groupLabel`, `theme`, `language` and `style: "none"` as in the other drawings. Four sizes: `card` (1080 by 1350), `square` (1080 by 1080), `wide` (1200 by 675, the finalists at the two ends) and `phone` (390 wide, as tall as the rounds need), listed in `ROAD_SIZES`. It gives back a chart's shape without layers: `options`, `title`, `summary`, `totals` (each finalist's record, the rounds, the champion), `draw(extra)` and `table()`, a row for every match. The SVG is `role="img"` with a title and a description, reads in the scene and every renderer, goes in a `drawGrid`, and has ids of its own; a team with no flag or logo is drawn as its initials on its colour. New: `KYOGIJO_ROAD_STYLE`, the types `RoadChart`, `RoadFinalist`, `RoadMatch`, `RoadGroupMatch`, `RoadFinal`, `RoadOptions`, `RoadScore`, `RoadDecided`, `RoadSize`, `RoadSizeInfo`, `RoadRecord` and `RoadTotals`, and the `road` words of `KYOGIJO_STRINGS` (`en` and `ja`; a custom set of words must give them).
- **A round team mark in a score** ([docs/score.md](./docs/score.md#round-marks)). `mark: "round"` on a team, or `markShape: "round"` on the score, draws its flag or logo clipped to a circle with a thin ring, on each side of the surface (the end zones of a pitch, the halves of a court), in the badge and in the strip of a compact card; where the words are turned (along the ends of a wide pitch, in a tall drawing) the mark is turned back and stays upright. A round mark with no logo is the team's initials on its colour; `markFit: "contain"` shows a crest whole on a plain disc. Off by default, and a drawing without it is the same text as before. New: `Team.mark`, `Team.markFit`, `ScoreOptions.markShape`, the type `TeamMark` and `KYOGIJO_MARK_STYLE` (`--ky-mark-ring`, `--ky-mark-edge`, `--ky-mark-plain`); a drawing writes the rules in only when it has a round mark.
- The demo has a "Road to the final" section with made-up nations (sixteen that do not exist, each with a simple flag drawn in code in `demo/road-data.js`), a play-off series, and the round mark on a pitch, in English and Japanese; the docs gallery has the same drawings, each made from the code beside it.

### Changed

- **The demo's Ballparks section is short, with filters, and there is a Soccer grounds section like it.** All 94 park cards in one grid made a section 2,200 px tall on a desk and 6,000 px on a phone. It now opens on the MLB parks in use today (30), as two rows of eight on a desk and one row that scrolls sideways on a phone (a row keeps the section to one screen, and the next card shows at the edge to say it scrolls), with "Show all 30 parks" under them: 640 px on a desk and 580 px on a phone. Above the cards are the league (All, MLB, NPB), whether a park is in use (All, Today, Past), the era (a decade, for the past parks), a search that reads every name a park has had, in English and in Japanese (`placesBy`: `Camden Yards`, `甲子園`, `ヤンキースタジアム`; the filters are off while it is typed, so it reaches every park), a count line in a polite live region (`30 of 94 parks`) and a reset. The grounds have the same: the grounds of the World Cup finals open (28 of 64), with group (finals, 2026, clubs), country and search, and the section is no taller. The filters are in the address under their own names (`parkleague`, `parkstatus`, `parkera`, `parkq`, `parkall`; `groundgroup`, `groundcountry`, `groundq`, `groundall`), written only when they are not the default. The build draws the default view of both sections into the HTML, so they are whole with scripts off and move nothing when the script takes over; the e2e tests hold that (a layout shift of 0 with the sections in view) and cover the filters, both searches, show all, the address, the phone and the desk. The package is unchanged.

### Fixed

- **The demo's first paint is whole.** The stage was a thin bar and every panel an empty box until the script ran, then the page jumped (a layout shift of 0.5). The build now draws the playground's first view into the HTML: the default rink, every English word, and the controls in their first state, so the page reads and shows its drawing with scripts off, and the script takes over the nodes that are there. The stage keeps its box from the first byte (its shape, by the drawing's width over its height), and an address that asks for another sport, size or way round has its box, rows and pressed choices set by a small inline script before anything is drawn, so the drawing swaps in place; a page opened in Japanese (the address, a kept choice or the browser) is held back, invisible but laid out, and shown in Japanese rather than English first. The docs, the gallery and the API reference have their header, footer and search words in the HTML too, and set `lang` before paint. The layout shift on load is 0 at every address and width tried (0.003 at most, for a past season whose notes are drawn by the script), and the e2e tests hold it under 0.05. The package is unchanged.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-10-14

### Added

- **Names for every park and ground** ([docs/ballparks.md](./docs/ballparks.md#names), [docs/grounds.md](./docs/grounds.md#names), [docs/references.md](./docs/references.md#names)). Each of the 94 ballparks and 64 grounds has `name`, the everyday name in English (the one English sports media use, with the current sponsor's where that is what people say); `nameJa`, the everyday name in Japanese (every place has one: the 74 ballparks of the United States and Canada, which a Japanese drawing used to name in English, now have the title of their Japanese article); `readingJa`, its reading in kana wherever it has kanji; `localName` and `localLanguage`, the everyday name in the language of the place; and `aliases`, `{ name, lang, kind, from?, to?, source?, revision?, reading? }`: 720 names, each with where it was read, of the kinds `wikipedia` (every article that was cited, English and Japanese, with its revision, so that no citation is lost), `official`, `sponsor`, `former` (with the years a source gives) and `nickname`. The evidence is Wikidata's labels, aliases and properties official name, nickname and name (CC0), read politely by `pnpm names:fetch` into `scripts/names.wikidata.json`, and the infobox and first paragraph of the articles each place cites; a name with no source stops the build. A ground's `names` (the names it had in some years) are as before.
- **Lookups by name**: `parkByName` and `parksByName` (`/parks`), `groundByName` and `groundsByName` (`/grounds`), and `placeByName` and `placesByName` (`/atlas`) find a place by any of its names, in English, in Japanese or in the language of the place, best match first (the everyday names, then the official, cited and sponsors' names, then the former names and the nicknames). The comparison, `plainName`, ignores case, accents, spaces and punctuation, full-width and half-width, hiragana and katakana, the long vowel mark (`ヤンキー・スタジアム` is `ヤンキースタジアム`), the old and new forms of ヂ, ヅ and ヴ, and a leading `The`. The atlas search (`placesBy({ text })`) reads the aliases and the readings too. `officialName`, `parkOfficialName` and `groundOfficialName` give the name a place officially has when it is not the everyday one.
- A drawing says the official name in its description when it is not the name it uses: `Its official name is Hanshin Koshien Stadium.`, `正式名称は阪神甲子園球場です。` (`said.officially` in `KYOGIJO_STRINGS`), in the year of the drawing where the official name is dated.
- The demo's Atlas detail panel lists the official name and the other names (`atlasOfficial`, `atlasAlso`), and its search finds them.

### Changed

- **Breaking: `name` is the everyday name, no longer the title of the article that was cited** (that title is an alias with kind `wikipedia`). Seven English names changed: `hanshin-koshien-stadium` is `Koshien Stadium` (was `Hanshin Koshien Stadium`), `mazda-stadium-hiroshima` `Mazda Stadium` (was `Mazda Zoom-Zoom Stadium Hiroshima`), `rakuten-mobile-park-miyagi` `Rakuten Mobile Saikyo Park Miyagi` (was `Rakuten Mobile Park Miyagi`, the name until 2025), `globe-life-park-in-arlington` `Choctaw Stadium` (as its article is titled now), `olympiastadion-munich` `Munich Olympic Stadium`, `olympiastadion-berlin` `Berlin Olympic Stadium`, and `groupama-stadium` `Groupama Stadium` (was `Parc Olympique Lyonnais`). `nrg-stadium` stays `Reliant Stadium`: its article says it was renamed back from `NRG Stadium` on 1 August 2026, and `NRG Stadium` (2014 to 2026) is a dated name and a `sponsor` alias.
- **Breaking: `nameJa` is the everyday name in Japanese**, as Japanese media say it where the Japanese article says which. Thirteen changed: `甲子園球場` (was `阪神甲子園球場`), `神宮球場` (was `明治神宮野球場`), `マツダスタジアム`, `エスコンフィールド北海道`, `楽天モバイル 最強パーク宮城`, `日産スタジアム` (was `横浜国際総合競技場`), `マラカナン`, `エスタディオ・モヌメンタル`, `ドイチェ・バンク・パルク`, `グルパマ・スタジアム`, `サン・シーロ`, `エティハド・スタジアム` and `カシマサッカースタジアム`; the old names are aliases (`official` or `wikipedia`). `ParkData.nameJa` and `Place.nameJa` are no longer optional: code that builds a `ParkData` or a `Place` of its own must give one.
- **Breaking: `Ground.nameLocal` is now `localName`**, and it is the everyday name in the language of the place (`San Siro` for the Meazza, `Maracanã`, `La Bombonera`, `Лужники`, `日産スタジアム`), not the name of the local article (the official ones are aliases). The parks of Japan, Montreal's two, and every ground outside the English-speaking world have `localName` and `localLanguage` too.
- **Breaking: the SVG written by `drawKyogijo` changes.** The English drawing of 15 grounds and 13 ballparks differs from 0.2.0's (the name, or the sentence that says the official name), and so does the Japanese drawing of every sport wherever it uses a word the native read of the Japanese settled (see below); every drawing's `data-version` reads `0.3.0`, which also changes the ids that are made from it. A stored copy of an old drawing, or a snapshot test of one, needs to be made again.
- **A native read of every Japanese string**, one terminology for the whole package: `ペナルティー` with the long vowel, as the JFA writes it (`ペナルティーエリア`, `ペナルティーアーク`, `ペナルティーマーク`, `ペナルティースポット`), `ウイング`, `本塁` for home plate (was `ホームベース`), `ゴーリー` for the goaltender, `国際標準` for the FIFA pitch, `BCプレイス`, `ゲーリック・ゲームズ`, `中堅正面` for dead centre, `ゴール裏` for behind the goal line, `フェアゾーン` and `ファウルゾーン`, `ゲームごとのスコア`; ranges are written with `〜` (was the full-width tilde `～`), and a size gives its unit once, `105×68m` (was `105m×68m`). English output is unchanged.
- The drawings that changed on purpose: the English drawing of 15 grounds and 13 ballparks (the name, or the sentence that says the official name) and the Japanese drawing of 8 grounds and 84 ballparks (the 74 of the United States and Canada, named in katakana now instead of in English, and 10 of Japan); a test holds every other drawing, in English and in Japanese, to the bytes it had.
- The demo's help text for the sport selector names all 20 sports, in English and in Japanese (it listed 14).
- The demo's Japanese status choices read as the status the drawing prints (`試合終了（延長）`, `試合終了（SO）`, `試合終了（延長戦）`, `試合終了（PK戦）`), and its Japanese page titles and labels follow the same terminology.

### Fixed

- **Spoken times in Japanese read as the time of day.** A replay's length and an event's time were said as `0:12` and `1:12`, which a Japanese reader takes for a clock; they are `12秒` and `1分12秒` now (`replaySpoken`, and the new strings `replay.spokenSeconds`, `replay.spokenMinutes` and `replay.spokenHours`, which a custom set of words must give). English still says `0:12`.
- **A convex hull's area unit was `平方m` in Japanese.** The unit is a word of the language now, `平方メートル`, `平方フィート` and `平方ヤード` (`team.squareUnits`, `team.squareOther`; `team.hullItem` takes the whole unit, `{unit}`, not `square {unit}`). English reads as it did.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-10-14

### Added

- **Layouts: small multiples** (`@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo/layouts`, [docs/layouts.md](./docs/layouts.md)). `drawGrid(cells, { columns, gap, title, subtitle, endnote, legend, width, height, preset, background, language, theme })` puts many drawings in one SVG, as mplsoccer's `grid` does for pitches: one per park, per period or per player, under a shared title and subtitle, over a credit line, with a key they share. A cell is the options of `drawKyogijo`, a drawing's SVG, a chart, or `{ drawing, label }`. Every drawing keeps its own title and description and gets ids of its own (the same drawing may appear twice, and each rule scoped to an id follows it); the group has a title and a description made from them; the base stylesheet is written once. `preset` fits a social card (`card`, 1200 by 675), a square post (`square`, 1080 by 1080) or a phone (`phone`, 390 wide, one column, as tall as the drawings need); the columns are the number that makes the drawings largest. The scene and every renderer read a grid like any drawing. English and Japanese words for the group's title and description, and a test of every sport, every preset, ids, bounds and renderers.
- **Backgrounds** (`background` in the options of a drawing, and of a grid). A pattern drawn in SVG, with no image and no random number (`felt`, `grass`, `wood`, `ice`, `paper`, `grid`, `dots`), in the drawing's own ink at a low opacity so that it follows light and dark, laid on the apron under the surface; a colour under it; and a photograph (`image`, held to the rules of a team's `logo`, `imageOpacity`, `imageFit`) as an `<image>` between the two. Without a background a drawing is byte for byte as before. The canvas renderer cuts a photograph that fills the ground (`slice`) at its edges.
- **Runnable gallery examples.** Every picture of the gallery has a link to download the drawing as an SVG file and an "Open in a page" link to one HTML file, made when the site is built, that shows the picture, runs the snippet against the package on jsDelivr (`@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo@<major>/+esm`, with the entries imported as `<major>/charts/+esm` and so on) and keeps the build's picture when the published package has no such feature. No third-party sandbox is used, and the page says that jsDelivr serves only published versions. A test runs every page's script against the source. A gallery page of layouts and backgrounds.
- **Lacrosse, rugby, Australian football, Gaelic games, cricket, field hockey and handball** (`sport: "lacrosse"`, `"rugby"`, `"afl"`, `"gaelic"`, `"cricket"`, `"fieldhockey"`, `"handball"`, [docs/field-sports.md](./docs/field-sports.md)). Each surface to scale from its own current rulebook, every figure cited, rule by rule, in docs/sources.md: World Lacrosse's men's and women's fields and box floor (2025 to 2028), the NCAA's men's and women's fields, the PLL's field (its 2024 book, the newest found) and the NLL's floor; World Rugby's Law 1 (2026), the NRL's (2023) and the IRL's (2024) playing fields; the AFL's Law 3 (2026), as an oval cut at its ends, with six named grounds at the length and width the AFL's venue pages print; the GAA's Rule 1 for football and hurling (2026); the pitch, creases and wickets of the Laws of Cricket (4th Edition 2026) in a ground with a boundary of your own and the ICC's 30-yard circle (October 2026); the FIH's pitch (March 2026) and the IHF's court (1 July 2025). 7 sports, 7 lacrosse leagues, 3 rugby, 2 Gaelic, 3 cricket (`LEAGUES`), the option `field` (`length`, `width`, `inGoal`, `ground`, `boundary`, `circle`: a size within the range each rulebook allows, an error that says the range when it is outside), `FIELD_SPORTS`, `fieldPlan` and `AFL_GROUNDS`.
- **The field sports' scores**: Australian football's `goals` and `behinds` written `10.12 (72)` and Gaelic games' `goals` and `points` written `2-14 (20)`, the total worked out; a cricket board of runs for wickets, overs and each innings (`wickets`, `overs`, `innings`, the status `innings-2`), with the sentences for each in the description.
- Ranges for the field sports (`in-goal`, `twenty-two`, `twenty`, `crease`, `forward-fifty`, `centre-square`, `large-rectangle`, `pitch`, `circle`, `twenty-three`, `goal-area`, with `RangeInfo.leagues` for a range that only some leagues mark), the zone sets `field-thirds` and `cricket-ground`, the frames `lacrosse`, `rugby`, `afl`, `gaelic`, `cricket`, `fieldhockey` and `handball` (the centre of the surface as the origin), landmarks, features (`FEATURES.rugby` and the others), the goal styles for lacrosse, field hockey and handball, `KYOGIJO_FIELDS_STYLE` (the colours of a handball floor and a hockey pitch), a gallery page and English and Japanese words for all of it. The other 13 surfaces' drawings are the same text as before.
- **Walls and quirks of a ballpark** (`walls`, `quirks`, `parkWallsIn`, `parkQuirksIn`, `Park.walls`, `Park.quirks`, [docs/ballparks.md](./docs/ballparks.md)). `walls: true` draws a band outside each stretch of a park's fence whose width and shade (four, light and dark, `KYOGIJO_WALLS_STYLE`) say how high it stands, from the height its article gives (35 parks, 24 of MLB and 11 of Japan: the Green Monster 37 feet 2 inches, the Little Green Monster, the Crawford Boxes, Oracle Park's 24 foot right field wall, the Baggie, Wrigley Field's ivy walls), for the season `year` names; `walls: { legend: true, labels: true }` adds a key and each wall's name and height, in feet or metres, in English or Japanese, and the description lists them. `quirks: true` draws Tal's Hill (2000 to 2016), the Polo Grounds' and Tiger Stadium's overhangs, Pesky's Pole, the Tiger Stadium flagpole, the Polo Grounds' bullpens, Wrigley Field's ivy and basket, the distance to the backstop (43 parks) and the sides of four parks' dugouts; `quirks: { only, labels }` chooses and names them. The element reads `walls` and `quirks`. The data is `walls`, `quirks`, `labels`, `track_ft`, `backstop_ft` and `dugouts` in `scripts/parks.data.json`, each at the revision the park already cites; the angles each stretch covers are our reading of the article's words (docs/references.md), a park whose article gives no figure keeps its drawing, and without the options every park's drawing is byte for byte what it was (a test holds the hash of 90).
- **More points of a fence, and a warning track.** Wrigley Field's two wells (9 points, not 5), Sportsman's Park's twelve markers (not 6) and Milwaukee County Stadium's nine (not 7) are read from their articles' text, each with its label in `Park.labels`; PNC Park's warning track is its published 18 feet and not the rule of thumb of 15. These four parks' drawings change; the other 90 do not.
- **Display ranges and zoom** (`range`, `RANGES`, [docs/ranges.md](./docs/ranges.md)). A drawing cropped to a named part of any of the thirteen surfaces, as sportypy's `display_range` and hockey_rink's partial rinks do, in one option: a rink's `offensive-zone`, `defensive-zone`, `neutral-zone` and `half`; a court's `half-court` and `paint`; a football field's `red-zone`, `between-the-20s`, `end-zone`, `field-of-play`, `own-half` and `opponent-half`; a pitch's `half`, `final-third`, `defensive-third`, `middle-third` and `penalty-area`; a ballpark's `infield` and `outfield`; a net sport's `half-court`; a curling sheet's `sheet`, `half` and `house` (which `sheet.show` and `sheet.end` keep saying); at the end `{ name, end }` asks for, or a box `{ x0, x1, y0, y1 }` in the drawing's frame, in `units` or in any registered frame. The view is cut with a small margin and the orientation is kept; data and heat layers are cut to the range and sized, laid out and binned against it, with the legend inside; `axes`, `landmarks` and `direction` stand against what is shown, on the apron beyond the cut; a score's panel is moved inside the range or, for one too small to hold it, becomes the strip under the drawing, and a team's name is written only where its place is shown. The description says what is shown, in English and Japanese, and the root carries `data-range`. `<kyogijo-field>` reads `range` and `range-end`. Without `range` the drawing is byte for byte what it was, and a test holds every named range of every sport, wide and tall, with data, annotations and a score.
- **Special games** (`host`, `placement` and `event` in the options, `@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo/events`, [docs/special-games.md](./docs/special-games.md)). One surface set inside another, to scale: the host (a ballpark of `PARKS`, a soccer ground or pitch, a football field, or a plain deck) drawn as it is alone, and the drawing's own surface set inside it through one transform, so that its lines, its team banners, its score and its data `layers` are the inner surface's, in its own frame. `EVENTS` holds 18 games in the order they were played, each with its day, its venue, its host, where the surface stood and its sources: the NHL's Winter Classics of 2009, 2010, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2022 and 2023, the Stadium Series of 2014 at Dodger Stadium and Yankee Stadium, the Heritage Classics of 2003, 2011, 2014, 2016, 2019 and 2023, the 2011 Carrier Classic on USS Carl Vinson, and MLB's London Series of 2019 and 2023 with their published fences. Where a source gives the rink's place (Wrigley Field 2009, Fenway Park 2023) it is used and cited; where none does the place is a convention (`midfield`, `infield`, `diamond`, `deck`, worked out by `conventionPlacement`) and each game says so. `eventOptions(id)` draws a game, `eventFit(id)` says whether its surface lies inside the host or how much of it lies beyond, and a drawing of a game says so in its description, in English and Japanese. A drawing with no host is the same string as before.
- **Renderers** ([docs/renderers.md](./docs/renderers.md)). The drawing as plain data and on other targets, from the code that writes the SVG, with no second drawing and no dependency added: `sceneOf` (`@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo/scene`) lists every shape in paint order with its colours worked out for light or dark, its matrix, clips, gradients and patterns, and `sceneToSvg` writes it as an SVG that needs no stylesheet; `drawToCanvas(ctx, drawing, { width, theme })` (`/canvas`) paints it on a browser's, an `OffscreenCanvas`'s or a Node canvas's context with the context typed by what is called; `appendSurface(selection, drawing, x, y)` and `surfaceDomain` (`/d3`) put it in a d3 selection by the caller's linear scales; `surfaceMark` and `surfaceScales` (`/plot`) make an Observable Plot mark and its scales; `sceneToGeoJSON(drawing, { frame })` (`/geojson`) writes each shape as an RFC 7946 feature in feet or a provider's frame; `toPNG(drawing, { width })` (`/png`) draws a PNG in Node through the optional peer `@resvg/resvg-js`, loaded when called, with an error that says what to install. The SVG tree gives every drawing back byte for byte (`parseSvg`, `writeSvg`), the style was compared with Chromium's `getComputedStyle` and the canvas with the browser's own drawing of the SVG (`e2e/renderers.demo.mjs`), and the sizes of the code are held to budgets.
- **Tennis, volleyball, badminton, pickleball and table tennis** (`sport: "tennis"`, `"volleyball"`, `"badminton"`, `"pickleball"`, `"tabletennis"`, [docs/courts.md](./docs/courts.md)). Each court to scale from its own rulebook, every figure cited in docs/sources.md: the ITF's Rules of Tennis (2026; the court, the service lines 21 feet from the net, the centre mark, the net posts 3 feet outside the doubles court, the singles sticks on request), the FIVB's Official Volleyball Rules and Official Beach Volleyball Rules (2025-2028; the centre line, the attack line 3 m from its axis, the front zones, the antennae, the free zone, the attack line's extension and the posts 1 m out for the FIVB's competitions; beach volleyball is `league: "fivb-beach"`), the BWF's Laws of Badminton (as amended 26 April 2025, with Diagram A), USA Pickleball's rulebook (2026; the non-volley zones and centerlines) and the ITTF's Laws of Table Tennis (the 2026 statutes; the table and the net). ### Changed

- **Breaking: the park status `"historic"` is now `"past"`.** `Park["status"]` is `"current" | "secondary" | "past"`, as for the grounds and the atlas; the six NPB parks that read `"historic"` read `"past"` (the type and the value in `PARKS`). Code that compares `park.status === "historic"` must compare with `"past"`.
- **Breaking: `Sport` and `SPORTS` grow from 6 to 20 sports.** The type was `"nfl" | "cfl" | "ifaf" | "nhl" | "nba" | "mlb"`; it now also has `"soccer"`, `"curling"`, the net sports (`"tennis"`, `"volleyball"`, `"badminton"`, `"pickleball"`, `"tabletennis"`) and the field sports (`"lacrosse"`, `"rugby"`, `"afl"`, `"gaelic"`, `"cricket"`, `"fieldhockey"`, `"handball"`). A TypeScript `switch` over `Sport` that is checked to be exhaustive must add a case for each, and code that loops over `SPORTS` now meets all twenty.
- **Breaking: the NHL trapezoid is 22 feet wide at the goal line, not 18.** `NHL.trapezoid.atGoalLine` was 18 feet; Rule 1.8 has 22 feet, the figure since 2014-15, when the trapezoid was widened by 2 feet each side (18 feet from 2005-06 to 2013-14, which `rulesFor("nhl", year)` and a drawing with `year` still give). The rink is drawn with the wider trapezoid by default.
- **Breaking: the NHL's hash marks are 5 feet 7 inches apart, not 5 feet 11 inches.** The marks beside an end-zone circle were drawn 5 feet 11 inches apart, which added the lines' width to a gap it does not enter; Rule 1.9 says 5 feet 7 inches (`NHL.hashApart`). The AHL, ECHL, OHL, WHL and PWHL, which share the NHL's Rule 1, change with it. docs/sources.md also records where the NHL's Rule 1.8 (a 22-foot trapezoid at the goal line, drawn) and Rule 27.8 (18 feet) disagree.
- **Breaking: MLB bases are 18 inches square, not 15.** `MLB.baseSize` was 15 inches; Rule 2.03 of the Official Baseball Rules has 18 inches from the 2023 season. The size is now a marking in `rulesFor("mlb", year)` (`baseSize`, 15 inches before 2023, 18 from 2023, with `eraChanges("mlb")` listing it), so the field is drawn with 18-inch bases by default and 15-inch ones for a season before 2023.
- **Breaking: the SVG written by `drawKyogijo` changes.** Every NHL drawing (the trapezoid and the hash marks) and every MLB drawing (the bases) differs from 0.1.0's, so a stored copy of an old drawing, or a snapshot test of one, needs to be made again. Every drawing's stylesheet is also about 2 kB larger.

### Fixed

- **NHL hash marks were 3 feet apart before 2014-15.** The rulebook of 2012-13 (Rule 1.9) says "three feet (3') apart", and the NHL and NHLPA release of 6 October 2014 moves them to 5 feet 7 inches for 2014-15. `rulesFor("nhl", year).rules.hashApart` is 3 feet through 2013-14 and 5 feet 7 inches after, `eraChanges("nhl")` lists both (`nhl.hashApart.2012`, `nhl.hashApart.2014`) and a rink drawn for an earlier season has the marks 3 feet apart. Seasons before 2012-13 are cited by no rulebook read, so they keep 3 feet and `gaps` names `hashApart`. The AHL, ECHL, OHL, WHL and PWHL are unchanged: no page read dates a change of their marks.
- **docs/sources.md cited the wrong baseball rule for home plate.** Home plate is Official Baseball Rules 2.02 (Home Base) in the 2024 edition; 2.03 is The Bases. The 90-foot square is 2.01, not 2.01 and 2.02.
