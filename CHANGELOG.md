## [Unreleased]

### Fixed

- **MLB bases are 18 inches square from 2023.** `MLB.baseSize` was 15 inches; Rule 2.03 of the Official Baseball Rules has 18 inches from the 2023 season. The size is now a marking in `rulesFor("mlb", year)` (`baseSize`, 15 inches before 2023, 18 from 2023, with `eraChanges("mlb")` listing it), so the field is drawn with 18-inch bases by default and 15-inch ones for a season before 2023.
- **NHL hash marks are 5 feet 7 inches apart.** The marks beside an end-zone circle were drawn 5 feet 11 inches apart, which added the lines' width to a gap it does not enter; Rule 1.9 says 5 feet 7 inches (`NHL.hashApart`). The AHL, ECHL, OHL, WHL and PWHL, which share the NHL's Rule 1, change with it. docs/sources.md also records where the NHL's Rule 1.8 (a 22-foot trapezoid at the goal line, drawn) and Rule 27.8 (18 feet) disagree.
- **NHL hash marks were 3 feet apart before 2014-15.** The rulebook of 2012-13 (Rule 1.9) says "three feet (3') apart", and the NHL and NHLPA release of 6 October 2014 moves them to 5 feet 7 inches for 2014-15. `rulesFor("nhl", year).rules.hashApart` is 3 feet through 2013-14 and 5 feet 7 inches after, `eraChanges("nhl")` lists both (`nhl.hashApart.2012`, `nhl.hashApart.2014`) and a rink drawn for an earlier season has the marks 3 feet apart. Seasons before 2012-13 are cited by no rulebook read, so they keep 3 feet and `gaps` names `hashApart`. The AHL, ECHL, OHL, WHL and PWHL are unchanged: no page read dates a change of their marks.
- **docs/sources.md cited the wrong baseball rule for home plate.** Home plate is Official Baseball Rules 2.02 (Home Base) in the 2024 edition; 2.03 is The Bases. The 90-foot square is 2.01, not 2.01 and 2.02.

### Added

- **A docs site** at `/docs/` of the demo site, made by `pnpm site` from `docs/*.md`: 31 pages in groups, a search box over every title and heading, the pages before and after, a copy button on every block of code, light and dark, phone width, and the contents in Japanese. The README keeps the pitch, the first minute, one example per area and the family block, and is about 37,000 characters instead of 64,000; its reference sections moved to [docs/](./docs/README.md) (getting started, surfaces, score and layout, soccer, ballparks, units, coordinate frames, accessibility, limits, entry points, architecture).
- **A gallery** in the docs site: every surface, league variant, season, park and ground sample, layer kind, heat layer and chart, each drawn by the build from the code shown beside it, with a copy button. A test runs every snippet.
- **How it compares** ([docs/compare.md](./docs/compare.md)): a table against sportypy, sportyR, `@sportsdataverse/sporty`, hockey_rink, mplsoccer, ggsoccer, GeomMLBStadiums, gavinr's spring-training parks and thestadi.com, from each README.
- **Coordinate frames and surface units as tables made from the code** (`pnpm docs:make`), with the same spot drawn in each of the 16 frames side by side.
- Every `ts` example of the README and of every docs page is run, and what it prints is held to its comment lines.

- **Ready-made charts** (`@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo/charts`, [docs/charts.md](./docs/charts.md)). One call each makes the layers and the drawing options for a chart, so a good chart takes one line and every layer can still be changed: `nbaShotChart` (made and missed shots and the six shot zones coloured by field-goal percentage, diverging from the league's), `nhlShotMap` (goals and shots sized by expected goals, `fold` to put both ends on one, slot zones or hexagons), `mlbSprayChart` (balls in play on the park of their season, past parks included, a mark for each hit type, balls beyond the fence drawn beyond it, spray zones by the side the batter bats from, Retrosheet codes as well as Statcast's `hc_x` and `hc_y`), `nflPassChart` (destinations by outcome and air yards, the line of scrimmage, the pass grid's completion percentages) and `soccerShotMap` with `goalMouth()` / `soccerGoalMouth` (shots sized by expected goals on the pitch or its half, a view of the goal frame with where each shot crossed the goal line, xG totals). Each chart has a key, a legend, a title and description for every layer and a table; the drawings are deterministic, held by fingerprints and size budgets; its words are in English and Japanese; every example on the page is run by a test. The tables of layers and heat layers now name the unit of a frame's own numbers (tenths of a foot for the NBA's) and not the drawing's. The demo has a Charts section.
- **Heat layers.** Five new kinds of data layer: `heatmap` (a binned statistic on a grid over the surface), `hexbin`, `zones` (named zones with aggregated values and labels), `contour` (a Gaussian kernel density, Scott's rule by default, drawn as filled bands or lines) and `legend`. Statistics are `count`, `sum`, `mean`, `median`, `max`, `min`, `percent`, `rate` (a field-goal percentage from made and missed) or a function, with `normalize`, `minCount`, a `domain`, a diverging `center` and cell or zone labels. Cells, hexagons and bands are cut to the surface's outline. Ten zone sets, each cited in docs/sources.md: soccer positional zones (juego de posición), thirds, channels and the 18 zones; the NBA's shot zones; the NHL's slot and home plate; baseball's spray zones and Retrosheet's sectors; football's pass grid. Five built-in colour ramps (`ember`, `ocean`, `mono`, `balance`, `tide`) with a light and a dark set, checked against every surface's colour; custom stops. Each layer has a title, a description that names its hottest cells or zones and, with `layerTable(layers, { surface })`, a table of its numbers. A 50 by 30 grid is held under 30 kB on every sport and 10,000 marks make a density in under 500 ms. The `/layers` entry exports the numbers (`binGrid`, `hexBins`, `densityGrid`, `contourRings` and more), `ZONE_SETS` and `buildZoneSet`. The demo's Data section shows a rink heat map, an NBA shot-zone chart with field-goal percentages, a soccer positional heat map and a density contour.
- **Six surfaces drawn to scale as SVG**: the NFL, CFL and IFAF football fields, the NHL rink, the NBA court and the baseball field, each from its rulebook or federation diagram, with every figure cited in docs/sources.md.
- **Score mode.** `drawKyogijo`, `drawScore` and `drawBadge` put each team's name and logo on its side (the end zones, the ends of the ice, the halves of the court, the dugouts) and the score in the middle, with the competition, the status, the date and an emphasis on the winner. Sizes `full`, `compact` and `badge`.
- **Wide, tall or fit the box.** `orientation: "landscape" | "portrait" | "auto"`: portrait turns the surface a quarter with every word upright and the teams top and bottom; `auto` follows `aspect`, and `mountKyogijo` watches the container and draws again when its shape changes.
- **Every ballpark.** `PARKS` has the 30 current MLB parks, 44 past ones, the 12 NPB home parks and eight other NPB grounds, each fence drawn from the distances published for it, with the article and revision each was read from and its Wikidata item (`wikidata`, `parkByWikidata`). A past park has the seasons it was used (`from`, `to`) and, where an article dates earlier fences, `periods`, chosen by the `year` option (`parkPointsIn`). `park`, `year` and `fence` options; names in English and Japanese.
- **Theming.** Every colour is a custom property in a light and a dark set, `colors` sets them from code, and a court has its own look (`court`) with five to start from in `COURT_THEMES`.
- **Four ways in.** The function, `mountKyogijo` for a container, the `<kyogijo-field>` custom element with parts, and a frame (`embed.html`) for a page that runs no script; `@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo/react` for React, which does not import it.
- **Accessible.** Each drawing is an `img` with a title and a description in English or Japanese.
- **Units.** `SURFACE_UNITS` names the unit each rulebook states its figures in; `convertLength`, `lengthsIn`, `dimensionsOf`, `surfaceSize` and `fenceIn` give every dimension in feet, inches, yards, metres, centimetres or millimetres; the `units` option says what the lengths of a drawing's options (`margin`, `fence`) are in.
- **Coordinate frames** (`@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo/frames`). `toSurface`, `fromSurface` and `convert` put a provider's points on a surface and move them between providers, with `units`, `anchors`, `limits` and `attacking`; the frames of the NHL API, HockeyTech, NBA stats, NFL tracking data, MLBAM and Retrosheet's hit locations; `registerFrame` and `piecewise` for the frames of other sports.
- **Soccer.** `sport: "soccer"` draws a pitch from Law 1 of the IFAB Laws of the Game at any size (`pitch`), with presets (international, UEFA, MLS, NCAA, the Laws' least and most, the FA's 9-, 7- and 5-a-side, FIFA's futsal), half pitches, six mowing patterns, four goal styles, corner flags, teams in their penalty areas and a score with extra time and penalties (`Team.penalties`, `final-aet`, `final-pens`). Ten data providers (`SOCCER_PROVIDERS`) as frames, a pitch drawn in a provider's own coordinates (`pitchType`) with an empty `ky-data` group for raw data, and `rescale` between any two, landmark to landmark.
- **Data layers** (`layers` in the options; `drawLayers`, `layerTable` and the `@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo/layers` entry). Points (seven shapes and team logos), lines, arrows (curved, wavy, open or filled heads, comet lines that fade along their length) and text over any surface, in the surface's feet, any unit or a provider's frame, cut to the boards, the fence or the court's lines with a clip path for each drawing, upright when the surface lies tall. Size by value, colour by value, team or outcome, filled and hollow outcomes (`OUTCOMES`), new colour properties (`--ky-layer`, `--ky-layer-home`, `--ky-layer-away`, `--ky-layer-edge`, `--ky-layer-hollow`, `--ky-layer-ink`, `--ky-layer-halo`) that follow a score drawing's teams. A title and a description for each layer, a tooltip for each mark and an HTML table of the data. 1,000 points add about 60 kB; 10,000 are drawn in about 20 ms. The demo has a Data section with a shot map, a pass map and a spray chart made from seeded, made-up data.
- **Surfaces through the years** (`@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo/eras`). `year` draws a surface as its league's rules marked it in a season, for the NHL, the NBA, the NFL, the CFL and baseball: the NHL's centre red line, goal lines, blue lines and trapezoid, the NBA's lane, three-point line and restricted area, the NFL's hash marks and goal posts, the CFL's end zones, hash marks and goal posts (and the field planned for 2027), the baseball pitcher's box and plate, batter's boxes and home plate. `rulesFor`, `eraChanges`, `eraStates`, `eraRange`, `seasonLabel` and `footballIn`; each change has the season it began in, a sentence in English and Japanese and its sources, and a marking no source covers is listed in `gaps` rather than guessed. The demo has a season slider.
- **Famous soccer grounds.** `GROUNDS` (`@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo/grounds`) has 64 grounds: the venue of every men's World Cup final from 1930 to 2022 and the 2026 final, all 16 venues of 2026, the venue of every Women's World Cup final, and well-known club grounds (both Wembleys, the Maracanã, Camp Nou, the Bernabéu, San Siro, Old Trafford, Anfield, Signal Iduna Park, the Allianz Arena, La Bombonera, Hampden Park, Celtic Park, the Estádio da Luz, the Parc des Princes, Saitama Stadium 2002, Nissan Stadium, the Seoul World Cup Stadium and more). Each has its city, country, Wikidata item and coordinates, the names it had with their years, its name in the place's language and in Japanese, its opening and closing years, capacity, the World Cups it staged, the pitch's length and width where its English Wikipedia article publishes them (42 of 64; the others are drawn at 105 by 68 m and marked `pitchSize: "default"`), and the article and revision each was read from. `drawKyogijo({ sport: "soccer", ground: "maracana", year })` draws the pitch at the ground's size under its name for the year; `groundById`, `groundByWikidata`, `groundNameIn`, `groundsHosting`, `groundSourceUrl`. `pnpm grounds` builds the data and docs/grounds.md; `pnpm grounds:check` asks Wikidata again. The demo has a ground picker.
- **A stadium atlas** (`@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo/atlas`). `PLACES` joins the 94 ballparks and 64 soccer grounds into one list with where each stands (`lat`, `lon`, Wikidata's P625), its country and city, its sport, leagues, whether it is in use today or of the past, and the years it was in use; `placesBy({ sport, kind, league, country, status, year, text })`, `placeById`, `atlasFacets` and `surfaceOf`. Every park now has its coordinates and the years it opened and closed (`pnpm parks:coordinates` fetches them, `pnpm parks:check` compares them with Wikidata). The demo has an Atlas: a chizu world map with a marker on every place (a circle for today, a diamond for the past), filters, a year slider, a list and the chosen place's surface beside the map; docs/atlas.md has a picture of the map, made by `pnpm site`.
- **League variants** (`@johnmorrisdotca/kyogijo/leagues`). `league` draws a surface as one league's rulebook marks it, with `sport` naming the family: basketball (the WNBA, FIBA, NCAA men and women, NFHS, the G League), hockey (the IIHF, NCAA men and women, AHL, ECHL, OHL, WHL, QMJHL, USHL, PWHL), football (the NCAA, NFHS 11, 9, 8 and 6 players, Texas six-man, the Indoor Football League) and baseball and softball (MiLB, NCAA, NFHS, Little League and its Intermediate division, three PONY divisions, NCAA and NFHS softball, USA Softball fast and slow pitch, WBSC slow pitch). Each league has its figures in its own unit, its name in English and Japanese, its sources with edition and address (`LEAGUES`, `VARIANTS`, `LEAGUE_SOURCES`, `leagueFigures`), and the changes a source dates (`leagueChanges`, `leagueAt`): the WNBA lane (2004), the NCAA restricted-area arc, the IIHF blue line and goalkeepers' trapezoid, the AHL blue lines (2005-06) and the NCAA men's trapezoid (2026-27). Score mode, themes, orientation, badges, `year` and data layers work on every league. The demo has a league picker; docs/variants.md lists every figure and docs/references.md what could not be cited.
- **The demo** with every sport and every park, wide and tall, light and dark, badges, compact cards, court looks, a live score, snippets for the four ways in, and the API reference.
